Subject: Reducing TTW composing-room costs

TTW can cut composing costs by an estimated 10% or more, and should authorise two tests to confirm it. Composition is the largest single production cost, and two independent levers are available: simplifying the process for inexpensive titles, and raising productivity by changing the composing method. Management disagrees about whether costs are high, but all parties are willing to investigate.

1. Simplify quality control on inexpensive titles

Every title passes through essentially the same quality-control stages, whether it is a complex reference book or a simple novel. TTW is uncompetitive on simple work, which suggests the stages are heavier than cheap titles require.

Recommended test: run selected jobs with fewer or differently timed checks and measure the effect on quality and on customers. George Kennedy has agreed to run this test. Potential saving: up to 10% of total composing cost.

2. Change the composing method to raise productivity

Industry comparisons put TTW 20–50% behind the benchmark on measured productivity.

Recommended test: a separate methods study to identify why productivity trails and which changes would close the gap.

3. Why the tests are necessary now

The department is overloaded, and manual composition is especially short of capacity. TTW pays below local market rates, cannot reliably hire or retain compositors, has just lost two employees, and now runs most jobs late. Overtime is more than 50% over budget while the union presses a new claim. Cost relief will not come from working the existing process harder.

What I have omitted, and why

  • Benchmarking against three other printers. Feasible, but a simple cost comparison will not settle whether TTW's costs are excessive, because the mix of complex and simple titles differs by printer. Not recommended as a next step.
  • The interview record (Roy Walter, Brian Thompson, George Kennedy) and the narrative of the two weeks' work. Available on request; only Kennedy's commitment to test bears on the recommendation.
  • The 40% hardback / 50–55% paperback cost shares. Already known to you; retained only as the basis for treating composition as the priority.

# Fixture 02: TTW composing costs

**Mode:** `write`
**Language:** `en`

## Context

TTW is a printing company in Aylesbury. A junior consultant spent two weeks
examining composing-room costs. The engagement manager already knows that
composition accounts for roughly 40% of hardback cost and 50-55% of paperback
cost, that TTW is uncompetitive on simple work, and that TTW does not know
whether its composing costs are actually excessive.

## Before

```text
To:
From:
Subject: TTW

This note records the information gathered during the past two weeks.

Composition is the largest production cost. Industry comparisons suggest weak
productivity and high cost at TTW. Every title, whether a complex reference
book or a simple novel, passes through essentially the same quality-control
stages.

People in Aylesbury want to understand the causes. I spoke with Roy Walter,
Brian Thompson, and George Kennedy. Kennedy is willing to test whether some
stages can be removed and why measured productivity trails the benchmark.

The department is overloaded. Manual composition is especially short of
capacity. TTW pays below local market rates, struggles to hire and retain
compositors, has just lost two employees, and now runs most jobs late.
Overtime is more than 50% over budget, while the union is pressing a new claim.

Conclusions

1. Costs may be reduced by:
   a. simplifying the process for inexpensive titles;
   b. increasing productivity by changing the composing method.

2. To simplify the process, TTW should test selected jobs and measure the
   effect of fewer or differently timed checks on quality and customers. The
   saving might reach 10% of total composing cost. A separate methods study
   could identify further productivity improvements because TTW trails the
   benchmark by 20-50%.

3. Comparisons with three other printers might provide useful context, although
   I doubt a simple comparison will settle the issue.

4. Managers disagree about whether costs are high, but all are willing to
   investigate.
```

Key line
  1. Eliminate unnecessary stages from the composing process for simple work.
  2. Raise wages to competitive levels.

The source's "simplify the process" and "increase productivity by changing the
method" are overlapping descriptions of the same process intervention. They
must not survive as two separate first-level branches. The missing second
branch is embedded in the observations: low wages cause vacancies, delays, and
overtime cost.

Levels

Under eliminating stages: every job follows the same process; TTW trails the
benchmark by 20-50%; run a controlled experiment; potential saving up to 10%.

Under raising wages: below-market pay; hiring and retention difficulty; two
departures; understaffing; late work; overtime more than 50% over budget; union
pressure.

Names, conflicting opinions, and generic competitor comparisons do not answer
the reader's question. They may be omitted with one explicit note.
