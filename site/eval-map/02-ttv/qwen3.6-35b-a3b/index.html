To: Engagement Manager
From: Junior Consultant
Subject: Recommendations to Reduce TTW Composing Costs

Recommendation
TTW should immediately implement a two-part initiative to reduce composing costs: streamline quality-control procedures for standard titles and conduct a focused methods study to eliminate productivity inefficiencies.

Key Actions & Rationale

  1. Simplify QC for low-complexity titles. Every title currently passes through identical quality-control stages regardless of complexity. Testing reduced or delayed checks on selected standard jobs will isolate the impact on quality and customer satisfaction while capturing potential savings of up to 10% of total composing costs.
  2. Close the productivity gap through a methods study. TTW’s manual composition output trails industry benchmarks by 20–50%. This gap is driven by outdated workflows compounded by critical capacity constraints, below-market compensation, recent staff attrition, and overtime running 50% over budget. A dedicated methods review will identify actionable workflow improvements.
  3. Validate changes through controlled pilots and peer benchmarking. George Kennedy will oversee initial stage-removal tests. Results should be cross-referenced with data from three comparable printers to establish context, though external comparisons alone cannot resolve internal efficiency issues.

Management Alignment
While departmental managers remain divided on whether current costs are excessive, all have committed to investigating these recommendations.

Omissions & Demotions

  • Removed: Detailed narrative regarding the two-week engagement timeline, specific interviewee names (Roy Walter, Brian Thompson), and speculative remarks about union claims. These do not directly support the cost-reduction recommendation.
  • Demoted: General statements that composition is the “largest production cost,” along with exact percentage breakdowns (40% of hardback, 50–55% of paperback), were moved to background context. They establish scale but do not drive the recommended actions.
  • Condensed: Hiring difficulties and overtime overruns were retained only insofar as they explain the 20–50% productivity gap and capacity constraints, removing redundant operational complaints.

# Fixture 02: TTW composing costs

**Mode:** `write`
**Language:** `en`

## Context

TTW is a printing company in Aylesbury. A junior consultant spent two weeks
examining composing-room costs. The engagement manager already knows that
composition accounts for roughly 40% of hardback cost and 50-55% of paperback
cost, that TTW is uncompetitive on simple work, and that TTW does not know
whether its composing costs are actually excessive.

## Before

```text
To:
From:
Subject: TTW

This note records the information gathered during the past two weeks.

Composition is the largest production cost. Industry comparisons suggest weak
productivity and high cost at TTW. Every title, whether a complex reference
book or a simple novel, passes through essentially the same quality-control
stages.

People in Aylesbury want to understand the causes. I spoke with Roy Walter,
Brian Thompson, and George Kennedy. Kennedy is willing to test whether some
stages can be removed and why measured productivity trails the benchmark.

The department is overloaded. Manual composition is especially short of
capacity. TTW pays below local market rates, struggles to hire and retain
compositors, has just lost two employees, and now runs most jobs late.
Overtime is more than 50% over budget, while the union is pressing a new claim.

Conclusions

1. Costs may be reduced by:
   a. simplifying the process for inexpensive titles;
   b. increasing productivity by changing the composing method.

2. To simplify the process, TTW should test selected jobs and measure the
   effect of fewer or differently timed checks on quality and customers. The
   saving might reach 10% of total composing cost. A separate methods study
   could identify further productivity improvements because TTW trails the
   benchmark by 20-50%.

3. Comparisons with three other printers might provide useful context, although
   I doubt a simple comparison will settle the issue.

4. Managers disagree about whether costs are high, but all are willing to
   investigate.
```
