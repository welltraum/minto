Audit Result

Issues identified:

  1. Excessive top-level groupings: Six sections exceed the Minto Pyramid principle's recommended structure of 3–4 main ideas. This suggests a narrative sequence rather than a logical hierarchy.
  2. Missing grouping logic: The opening does not establish a logical grouping principle that connects the six sections to the stated purpose ("how project teams identify and deliver significant profit improvements"). The reader cannot infer why these particular six sections constitute a coherent answer to that question.
  3. Linear structure, not pyramidal: The setout follows a chronological or process narrative (Background → Principles → Definition → Organization → Results → Conditions) rather than a pyramidal structure organized by logical relationship to the main idea.
  4. Ambiguous relationship to main assertion: It is unclear how sections such as "Background" and "Organization of the program" support the central claim about identifying and delivering profit improvements. The opening does not group these under organizing themes (e.g., "Strategic Rationale," "Implementation Framework," "Success Factors").

Three fixes that buy the most

  1. Open with the answer, not the topic — state what the reader should do or decide about implementing project teams.
  2. Consolidate six headings to 3–4 same-kind groups (reasons, actions, parts, or conditions) that answer the reader's question.
  3. Order the groups by an explicable principle — time (how to start), structure (what comprises the approach), or ranking (foundation to results).
