Audit: Period Graph Books Against Minto Pyramid Principle

Diagnosis

Primary Defect: Inverted Structure
The document begins with context (the production process) rather than the conclusion. The key message—"The production process badly needs streamlining"—is buried in paragraph 3 of the "Overall assessment." The pyramid is upside down: supporting details come first, the answer comes last.

Secondary Defect: No Recommendation
The document ends with a question ("Could we discuss how best to proceed?") rather than a clear recommendation or statement of what decision is needed. This violates the principle that the top of the pyramid must answer the reader's unstated question. The reader knows there are problems but not what action the writer proposes.

Tertiary Defect: Weak Horizontal Logic
The "Issues" section lists problems without grouping them by cause or by the type of corrective action required. The document identifies fragmentation and unclear responsibility as root causes but doesn't organize the supporting details (the specific errors and inconsistencies) into categories that would justify a particular solution.

What Must Change
  1. Lead with the conclusion: Open by stating that the production process requires streamlining and reorganization—not with a description of the current process.
  2. Add a clear recommendation: Replace "Could we discuss how best to proceed?" with a specific proposal (e.g., "We recommend consolidating data control under a single owner" or "We need to pause production while redesigning the workflow").
  3. Regroup the issues: Organize problems by their cause or by the solution they point to, rather than listing them as a narrative description. This supports the recommendation at the top.
  4. Reverse the sequence: Move "Overall assessment" to the beginning (as the conclusion), then provide "Production" and "Issues" as supporting evidence.

# Fixture 03: Period Graph Books

**Mode:** `audit`
**Language:** `en`

## Context

Corporate Financial Analysis recently took over production of four monthly
graph books used in presentations to senior management. Management knows that
errors and inconsistencies have appeared and asked for corrective proposals.

graph-presentation books previously handled by Corporate Planning. This memo

   the graphs.
4. Check the data and calculations.

Expected replacement structure

Reader question: What changes should we make to prevent unreliable graphs?

Top: Remove the opportunities for error across data transfer, graph-point
generation, and final presentation changes.

Key-line kind: changes/actions.

  1. Create a direct data link to the corporate computer.
  2. Create a reliable computerized routine for graph-point generation.
  3. Require presenter changes to be revalidated before use.
