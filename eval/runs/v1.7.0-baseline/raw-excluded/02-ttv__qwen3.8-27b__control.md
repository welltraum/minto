# TTW Composing-Room Costs

**To:**  
**From:**  
**Subject:** TTW composing-room costs

## Key line
TTW should reduce composing costs by (1) testing a simpler quality-control process for inexpensive titles and (2) commissioning a methods study to improve composing productivity. Together, these actions will establish whether TTW's costs are excessive and identify measurable savings.

## Situation
Composition is TTW's largest production cost, accounting for roughly 40% of hardback cost and 50-55% of paperback cost. TTW is uncompetitive on simple work.

## Complication
TTW does not know whether its composing costs are excessive. The current process applies essentially the same quality-control stages to every title, from complex reference books to simple novels. Measured productivity trails the benchmark by 20-50%. The department is overloaded, manual composition is especially short of capacity, most jobs are running late, and overtime is more than 50% over budget. Staffing conditions are increasing cost risk: TTW pays below local market rates, has struggled to hire and retain compositors, has just lost two employees, and the union is pressing a new claim.

## Supporting points
### 1. Simplify quality control for inexpensive titles
Select representative jobs and test fewer or differently timed checks. Measure the effect on quality, customers, and cost. The saving might reach 10% of total composing cost.

### 2. Improve productivity through a methods study
Commission a separate methods study to examine changes to the composing method. The 20-50% productivity gap against the benchmark indicates room for further improvement.

### 3. Scope the work so it remains decision-useful
Use comparisons with three other printers as context only; a simple comparison is unlikely to settle whether TTW's costs are excessive. Managers disagree about whether costs are high, but all are willing to investigate. George Kennedy is willing to test whether some stages can be removed and why measured productivity trails the benchmark.
