## Headings audit — Fixture 07

**Verdict:** the opening fails the pyramid at the top. It announces a subject, not an answer, and the setout is a topic list rather than a structure of ideas.

**1. No governing thought.** "This memorandum describes how project teams identify and deliver significant profit improvements" names the subject and withholds the point. The reader is told what the document is *about*, never what it *says*. Nothing above the six sections can be supported or challenged, so the six sections summarize nothing.

**2. No question in the reader's mind.** There is no situation–complication that raises a question the memorandum then answers. Without it, the reader has no reason to accept the six-part order as necessary rather than arbitrary.

**3. Headings are topic labels, not ideas.** All six are noun phrases ("Background," "Definition of project work," "Conditions required for success"). None makes an assertion, so none can be a member of a logical grouping. A reader who reads only the setout learns the document's table of contents and none of its thinking.

**4. The six items are a laundry list, not a group.** They are not mutually exclusive and they do not add up to a single line of argument:
- "Principles of the project-team approach" and "Definition of project work" overlap; a definition of the work is part of the approach.
- "Organization of the program" is a subordinate mechanic of the approach, not its peer.
- "Background" is context, not a supporting idea, and sits at a different level from the rest.
- "Distinctive benefits and results" and "Conditions required for success" are evaluative judgments; the preceding four are descriptive. Two logical categories are held at one level.

**5. No discernible logical order.** The sequence is neither deductive, nor time-ordered, nor structural, nor comparative in degree. It reads as the order in which the material was gathered.

**6. Six is the wrong count at one level.** Even if the items were genuine ideas, six unsummarized points at the top level exceed what a reader can hold. The overlaps in point 4 indicate the list should collapse into two or three summarized groups — but which ones cannot be determined from the visible text.

**Not assessable:** whether the body supports any of these headings, and what the actual governing thought should be. The body is unavailable and no claim about it is made here.
