To: Engagement Manager
From: [Consultant]
Subject: TTW composing costs — reducing cost by an estimated 10%, with more likely available

**TTW can cut composing costs, and should now run two studies to confirm how much.** Composing is 40% of hardback and 50–55% of paperback cost, so it is the only production cost large enough to restore competitiveness on simple work. Two levers are available:

**1. Simplify the process for inexpensive titles (saving up to 10% of total composing cost).**
Every title passes through essentially the same quality-control stages, whether it is a complex reference book or a simple novel. Simple titles therefore carry checking they do not need. Recommended action: run a controlled test on selected jobs, removing or re-timing checks, and measure the effect on quality and on customers.

**2. Raise productivity by changing the composing method (size not yet quantified).**
TTW trails the industry benchmark by 20–50%. Recommended action: a separate methods study to identify the achievable improvement and the investment required.

**Both studies can start now.** George Kennedy is willing to test whether stages can be removed and to investigate why measured productivity trails the benchmark. Managers disagree about whether costs are in fact high, but all are willing to investigate — so the studies will settle the question that TTW itself cannot currently answer.

**Capacity pressure makes the timing urgent.** The department is overloaded and manual composition is especially short of capacity. TTW pays below local market rates, cannot hire or retain compositors, has just lost two employees, and now runs most jobs late. Overtime is more than 50% over budget and the union is pressing a new claim. Delay raises cost through overtime and late delivery, and narrows the options available.
