**RECOMMENDATION**

**Extend Fieldbook to North and Islands in Q1, provided the program office implements three corrective actions before go-live:**

1.  **Restructure onboarding** to guarantee all technicians receive training prior to activation, eliminating the adoption lag observed in South and West.
2.  **Execute critical data integrations** to resolve the asset-register gap and invoicing code mismatch, preventing rework and warranty errors.
3.  **Assign an integration owner** to activate vendor solutions and establish a feedback mechanism to ensure lessons from Central are applied immediately.

---

### 1. The economic case for extension is strong, as proven ROI and efficiency gains outweigh incremental costs when adoption is optimized.

*   **Proven return on investment.** Fieldbook paid back its running costs within the second quarter in Central and East. South and West reached break-even despite slower adoption.
*   **Significant operational efficiencies.** Across live regions, completed jobs per technician rose from 4.6 to 5.3 (+15%), average travel time fell 18%, and same-day emergency insertion now averages 4 minutes. Quarter-over-quarter, overtime spending dropped 12% and fuel cost per job fell 9%.
*   **Extension economics are favorable under controlled conditions.** Extending to North and Islands adds £13,500 per month in licences and requires winter travel for training. The economics remain favourable if the adoption pace of Central and East is reproduced; a repeat of the West pattern would render the new regions unprofitable for at least two quarters.

### 2. All major technical and adoption risks are identified and have defined vendor solutions, removing uncertainty about platform capability.

*   **Adoption lag is caused by training logistics, not platform resistance.** South and West lagged because the two-day classroom course ran only once per region, causing late hires to miss onboarding. Coordinators' improvised shadowing arrangements consumed an estimated 380 technician-hours of lost route time. Dispatchers achieved 100% adoption immediately when the old spreadsheet was switched off, demonstrating that forced transition drives uptake.
*   **Data defects create rework and error risk, but fixes are ready.** Two gaps impair operations:
    *   *Missing asset history:* Technicians lack service history, leading to unnecessary part replacements in 9% of audited jobs and increased back-office calls.
    *   *Invoicing code mismatch:* Job-completion codes differ between systems, forcing the billing team to re-key ~300 records weekly and introducing transcription errors.
    *   *Solution:* The vendor catalogue lists supported connectors for both the asset register and invoicing import format. IT confirms these are straightforward. A configurable mapping table for codes exists and can be deployed in roughly one day.
*   **Vendor relationship is reliable and responsive.** Response times are within contract. Both known system gaps have vendor solutions waiting on Kestrel decisions rather than vendor work.

### 3. Program governance must evolve to protect the economics of expansion by capturing early lessons and closing ownership gaps.

*   **Early warnings were lost due to weak feedback loops.** Every negative pattern in this review was visible by week four of the Central go-live, yet the same issues replayed in subsequent regions. The program lacked a mechanism to convert field observations into program-level fixes. A formal feedback channel must be established before North and Islands launch.
*   **Integration ownership is currently undefined.** Despite the availability of vendor solutions, the mapping table for invoicing codes has not been scheduled because no Kestrel employee owns the integration. An owner must be assigned to trigger vendor action and scope the remaining integration work, which was excluded from the original rollout programme.
*   **Support desk burden reflects onboarding failures.** 44% of support tickets are password resets concentrated among technicians who missed the classroom course. The support desk is effectively performing training functions at ticket costs. Restructuring onboarding will reduce this drain and allow the desk to focus on genuine system issues.
