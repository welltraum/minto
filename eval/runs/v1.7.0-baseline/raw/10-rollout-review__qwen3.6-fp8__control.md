**Subject:** Recommendation to Extend Fieldbook to North and Islands Regions in Q1

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

**Recommendation:** Extend Fieldbook to the North and Islands regions in Q1, contingent upon restructuring technician onboarding and deploying two integrations prior to launch.

**Rationale:**
1.  **Financial Viability:** The business case for extension is sound provided adoption rates mirror Central and East; current rollout economics show positive ROI in successful regions.
2.  **Risk Mitigation:** All critical barriers to success are identified and have immediate, low-effort solutions available from the vendor and IT.
3.  **Proven Value:** Fieldbook delivers significant, measurable gains in productivity, cost reduction, and emergency response that justify scaling to remaining regions.

---

## 1. FINANCIAL CASE SUPPORTS EXPANSION IF ADOPTION TARGETS ARE MET

The economics of extending Fieldbook are favorable, assuming we replicate the adoption performance of Central and East.

*   **Current Return on Investment:**
    *   Central and East regions achieved payback on running costs within the second quarter.
    *   South and West are currently at break-even; slower adoption delayed their return to parity.
    *   Running costs for four regions total £31,000 per month.
*   **Extension Economics:**
    *   Extending to North and Islands adds £13,500 per month in licence fees and requires winter travel for training.
    *   If North and Islands achieve Central/East adoption pace, the extension will be profitable.
    *   A repeat of the West adoption pattern would result in negative economics for at least two quarters.
*   **Cost Savings Realized:**
    *   Overtime spending in live regions is down 12% quarter-over-quarter.
    *   Fuel cost per job is down 9%, consistent with route optimization.
    *   Unplanned costs to date include 380 technician-hours of lost route time due to improvised shadowing in South/West and £8,400 for a temporary billing clerk over two quarters.

## 2. CRITICAL RISKS HAVE IMMEDIATE SOLUTIONS

Two systemic issues and one process gap threaten adoption and data integrity; all have defined remedies requiring no vendor development.

*   **Restructure Technician Onboarding:**
    *   *Issue:* Reliance on a single two-day classroom course per region caused adoption lag in South and West. Late hires and absentees learned second-hand, leading to shadowing arrangements that pulled experienced staff off routes.
    *   *Evidence:* South took 11 weeks to pass 80% order volume; West remains at 71% after nine weeks. 34 of 118 West technicians never attended a session.
    *   *Solution:* Restructure onboarding to include additional sessions or targeted webinars for late hires. Eliminate reliance on shadowing. The support desk already runs weekly webinars; attendance correlates strongly with faster adoption.
*   **Deploy Vendor-Supported Integrations:**
    *   *Issue:* Fieldbook lacks feeds from the legacy asset register and invoicing system, causing warranty blind spots, unnecessary part replacements (9% error rate), and manual billing re-keying (300 records/week).
    *   *Solution:* The vendor catalogue includes connectors for both systems. IT confirms implementation is straightforward. A configurable mapping table for job-completion codes exists and can be deployed in approximately one day.
    *   *Action:* Scope these integrations immediately. The vendor relationship is strong, and solutions are waiting on Kestrel decisions, not vendor work.
*   **Implement Early Feedback Mechanism:**
    *   *Issue:* Negative patterns were visible by week four of the Central go-live but were not converted into program-level fixes, causing issues to replay in subsequent regions.
    *   *Solution:* Establish a formal process to capture field observations and trigger rapid remediation before regional rollouts.

## 3. OPERATIONAL BENEFITS ARE PROVEN AND SCALABLE

Fieldbook delivers substantial efficiency gains across the four live regions, validating the platform's value for North and Islands.

*   **Productivity and Throughput:**
    *   Completed jobs per technician per day increased from 4.6 to 5.3.
    *   Average travel time between jobs fell by 18% due to route-aware assignment.
    *   Same-day emergency insertion is now automatic, reducing median response time to 4 minutes.
*   **Adoption and Usage:**
    *   412 of 486 field technicians (85%) have activated accounts.
    *   83% of work orders are created and closed in Fieldbook.
    *   Dispatcher adoption is complete: all 41 dispatchers use Fieldbook scheduling from week one following the shutdown of the legacy spreadsheet.
*   **Data Quality and Reporting:**
    *   Fieldbook is the source of record for job status; regional managers utilize the dashboard for Monday reviews.
    *   Data quality is generally high, though resolution of the integration gaps noted above will eliminate duplicate customer records (currently ~70 merged per week) and reconcile job-count discrepancies between reporting and invoicing systems (currently 2–4% variance).
*   **User Behavior Notes:**
    *   Parts ordering via phone persists despite the Fieldbook screen functioning correctly; this is attributed to habit rather than defect and is expected to normalize with continued use.
