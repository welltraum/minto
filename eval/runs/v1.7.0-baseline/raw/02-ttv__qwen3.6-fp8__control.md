# Fixture 02: TTW composing costs

**To:**  
**From:**  
**Subject:** Reduce composing costs by simplifying processes and improving productivity  

**Key Message:**  
I recommend reducing composing costs by simplifying quality control for simple titles and conducting a methods study to improve productivity. These actions target the primary drivers of excess cost and offer potential savings of up to 10% plus further gains from closing the productivity gap.

**Recommendations:**

1. **Simplify the process for inexpensive titles.**  
   - All titles currently undergo identical quality-control stages, regardless of complexity.  
   - Test selected jobs with fewer or differently timed checks to measure effects on quality and customers.  
   - Potential saving: Up to 10% of total composing cost.  
   - George Kennedy is willing to perform these tests.

2. **Conduct a methods study to increase productivity.**  
   - TTW trails the industry benchmark by 20–50%, resulting in overtime exceeding budget by 50% and frequent delivery delays.  
   - A separate methods study will identify further productivity improvements.  
   - This addresses the root cause of our uncompetitive position on simple work.

**Supporting Information:**  
- Composition is the largest production cost.  
- Industry comparisons confirm weak productivity and high costs at TTW relative to peers.  
- Comparisons with three other printers will provide useful context, although a simple comparison alone will not settle the issue.  
- Managers disagree on whether costs are excessive, but all are willing to investigate.

**Material Omitted:**  
- Names of interviewees other than George Kennedy (Roy Walter, Brian Thompson).  
- Narrative details regarding staff interest in understanding causes.  
- Operational distress signals not directly addressed by the recommendations: below-market pay, recruitment and retention struggles, recent staff losses, union claims, and specific capacity shortages in manual composition.
