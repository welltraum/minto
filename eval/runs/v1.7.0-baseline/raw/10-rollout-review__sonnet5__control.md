# FIELDBOOK ROLLOUT — REVIEW FOR THE Q1 EXTENSION DECISION

## Recommendation

**Extend Fieldbook to North and Islands in Q1 — but only after fixing the onboarding model that caused South and West to underperform, and after assigning owners to two known, vendor-ready system fixes.** Done this way, extension should replicate the Central/East payback (full cost recovery within one quarter); done as before, it risks the West outcome — two quarters underwater.

This rests on three points:

1. **The platform works and pays for itself — when onboarding is done right.** Central and East reached full adoption in six weeks and profitability within the second quarter.
2. **Where adoption was slow, the cause was a single fixable design flaw in training delivery, not the product.** The same flaw is avoidable in North and Islands.
3. **Two data-integration gaps are quietly taxing every region, but both have vendor-ready solutions blocked only by the lack of a Kestrel owner.** They should be assigned before scaling further.

---

## 1. The platform delivers real gains when adoption goes well

- Completed jobs per technician per day rose from 4.6 to 5.3; travel time between jobs fell 18%.
- Emergency job insertion dropped from a manual phone chase to a 4-minute automatic process.
- Overtime is down 12% and fuel cost per job down 9% quarter over quarter in the four live regions.
- Central and East, which ran onboarding cleanly, hit full adoption in six weeks and had paid back the rollout's running cost by the second quarter.

**Conclusion:** the technology and the economics are sound; the variable is onboarding execution, not the product.

## 2. Slow adoption in South and West traces to one root cause: a single, non-repeated training session

- Both regions ran the two-day classroom course exactly once. Technicians hired afterward, or absent that day, never received formal training — 34 of 118 in West alone.
- This one gap cascades through the rest of the review:
  - It explains South's eleven-week and West's stalled (71% after nine weeks) adoption.
  - It explains 44% of the 1,940 support tickets (password/login issues from technicians who never logged in during the supported window).
  - It explains most webinar attendance, which correlates with the regions that missed classroom slots.
  - It forced improvised shadowing that cost an estimated 380 technician-hours of lost route time in South and West.
- Dispatchers, by contrast, adopted instantly in all four regions — because the old scheduling spreadsheet was switched off with no fallback. This shows that a hard cutover with no alternative drives fast adoption; a one-time optional-feeling course does not.

**Conclusion:** before extending to North and Islands, repeat the classroom course on a recurring schedule (or align it with a hard cutover of the paper/phone process) rather than running it once. This is the single highest-leverage fix available before Q1, and it is entirely within the program office's control.

## 3. Two unresolved system gaps are taxing every live region, and both have known fixes waiting only on ownership

These two issues surface independently in adoption, operations, support, finance, and reporting, but they are only two root problems:

**a. No feed from the legacy asset register into Fieldbook**
- Technicians open jobs with no equipment history, leading to 9% of audited jobs having parts replaced that were already replaced under warranty.
- It generates a steady stream of support calls and an unusable audit trail for dispute resolution.
- Requested from the asset-register team in month two; still unactioned. The vendor's connector for this feed already exists in its integration catalogue but has never been evaluated.

**b. Job-completion codes in Fieldbook don't match the invoicing system**
- Billing manually re-keys ~300 records/week, causing transcription errors that reach customers on invoices.
- This required hiring a temporary clerk (£8,400 for two quarters).
- It causes reporting and invoicing job counts to disagree by 2–4% weekly, reconciled manually at month end.
- The vendor has a configurable mapping table that takes about a day to set up — it hasn't been scheduled because no one on Kestrel's side owns the integration.

**Conclusion:** both gaps are inexpensive, vendor-supported fixes blocked purely by the absence of an assigned owner on Kestrel's side. Neither needs to be resolved before Q1 to justify extension, but both should be assigned now — they will only compound as two more regions go live.

## 4. Extension economics

- Fieldbook currently costs £31,000/month across four regions; adding North and Islands adds £13,500/month plus winter travel costs for classroom training.
- Payback is favorable if Central/East-style adoption is reproduced; a repeat of the West pattern would leave the new regions at or below break-even for at least two quarters.
- The fix in Point 2 (recurring training, not a one-time session) is the lever that determines which outcome North and Islands get.

## 5. A standing gap worth closing regardless of the Q1 decision

Every problem in this review — the training gap, the asset-register gap, the coding mismatch — was visible by week four of the very first rollout (Central) but had no mechanism to reach the program level before it repeated in each subsequent region. Recommend the program office add a checkpoint after each region's first month to capture and act on emerging issues before the next region goes live. The vendor relationship itself is not a concern: response times are within contract, and both system gaps are waiting on Kestrel decisions, not vendor delivery.
