**Subject:** Pilot simplified composition for inexpensive titles and test alternative methods to cut composing costs by up to 10 %

**Situation** – TT & W’s composing stage accounts for ≈ 40 % of hard‑back and 50‑55 % of paperback cost. Industry benchmarks show TT & W’s productivity 20‑50 % below peers, indicating weak productivity and high cost.

**Complication** – All titles, simple and complex, run through the same quality‑control stages despite limited capacity. Manual composition is overloaded, overtime exceeds budget by > 50 %, and two compositors have recently left, threatening timely delivery.

**Question** – Are TT & W’s composing costs excessive, and what should we do to bring them in line with the market?

**Answer** – We recommend **piloting a simplified composition process for inexpensive titles and concurrently evaluating alternative composing methods**; the pilot can deliver up to a 10 % reduction in total composing cost and identify further productivity gains.

---

### Why this recommendation works  

1. **Cost & productivity gap is real**  
   * Composing is the largest production cost (40‑55 % of book cost).  
   * TT & W trails benchmark productivity by 20‑50 %.  

2. **Pilot is feasible and low‑risk**  
   * Existing overload means any reduction in checks will free capacity.  
   * Selected simple titles can be tested without disrupting complex reference books.  
   * Overtime spend is already high; a pilot can immediately curb excess spend.  

3. **Potential upside**  
   * Early results suggest a **10 % saving on total composing cost** for the pilot set.  
   * A follow‑up methods study can uncover additional productivity improvements beyond the pilot.  

---

### What we need now  

* **Approve the pilot** – select a representative sample of inexpensive titles, define reduced‑check workflow, and set a 4‑week measurement period.  
* **Assign ownership** – compositing lead to design the simplified process; analytics lead to capture productivity and quality metrics.  
* **Decision deadline** – sign‑off by **[date, e.g., 15 May 2024]** to start before the next production cycle.
