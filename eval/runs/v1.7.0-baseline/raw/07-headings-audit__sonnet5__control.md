## Audit

**Issue: Headings are topic labels, not ideas.**
Every entry in the list (Background, Principles of the project-team approach, Definition of project work, Organization of the program, Distinctive benefits and results, Conditions required for success) names a subject but asserts nothing. The Minto Pyramid Principle requires that headings/section titles be complete thoughts — statements that tell the reader the point being made — not category labels. As written, a reader cannot tell what the memorandum actually claims about each topic, only what it will "discuss."

**Issue: No governing idea precedes or unifies the list.**
The introductory sentence ("This memorandum describes how project teams identify and deliver significant profit improvements") states a purpose, not a conclusion. The six headings are then presented as a flat inventory of contents rather than as support points answering a "why" or "how" question raised by a single main point. There is no visible top-line message that the six sections collectively prove or explain.

**Issue: No apparent logical grouping or order.**
The six items read as a sequence of report sections (background → principles → definitions → organization → benefits → conditions), which is a narrative/chronological structure, not a pyramid grouped by argument (e.g., situation–complication–resolution, or grounds supporting one conclusion). Because the underlying logic isn't visible, it cannot be confirmed whether the items are mutually exclusive and collectively exhaustive, or whether they are ordered to build an argument rather than just narrate document structure.

**Not assessed:** The body of the memorandum is unavailable, so whether the content under each heading actually supports a single governing thought, and whether the headings' order reflects deductive or inductive grouping, cannot be evaluated. No claims are made about the missing body.
