Subject: Reducing TTW composing costs

TTW should begin a focused cost-reduction programme by simplifying quality-control for selected simple titles and conducting a methods study to raise composing productivity.

This action is justified because TTW applies essentially the same control process to simple novels and complex reference works, despite being uncompetitive on simple work. Controlled trials should remove or retime selected checks, then measure effects on quality and customer acceptance. The potential saving is up to 10% of total composing cost.

TTW should also investigate how to change its composing method. Its measured productivity is 20–50% below the benchmark, indicating significant potential for improvement beyond the process-trial savings.

The work is practical to start now: Roy Walter, Brian Thompson, and George Kennedy support investigating the issue, and Kennedy is prepared to test removable stages and the productivity gap. The investigation should establish the size and causes of excess cost before TTW makes broader operational changes.

Omitted or demoted:

- General statements that composition is a large share of production cost.
- The proposed comparison with three other printers, since it is unlikely to establish causes or determine action.
- Staffing, pay, recruitment, overtime, lateness, and union-claim details; these may be relevant to a later capacity or labour-relations review but do not support the immediate recommendation.
