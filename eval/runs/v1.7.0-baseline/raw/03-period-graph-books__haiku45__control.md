# Audit: Period Graph Books Against Minto Pyramid Principle

## Defects Identified

**1. Missing Apex / No Clear Main Idea at Top**
The memo opens with a neutral statement of scope ("This memo describes issues") rather than a conclusion or recommendation. The actual main idea—that the production process requires streamlining and consolidation—is buried in the final "Overall assessment" section.

**2. Inverted Structure**
The document presents supporting details (Production process, then Issues) before stating the conclusion. Pyramid structure requires the main idea first, then supporting logic below. Currently, readers must work through the entire memo to discover what the author thinks should happen.

**3. Weak Termination**
The memo ends with a question ("Could we discuss how best to proceed?") rather than a clear recommendation or call to action. The pyramid apex should answer the reader's key question directly.

**4. Vertical Logic Failure**
The "Production" section describes a 4-step process, but its relationship to the "Issues" section is implicit. The reader must infer that the process structure *explains* why problems occur; this connection should be made explicit in the pyramid structure.

**5. Horizontal Logic Weakness**
The "Issues" section lists problems as a fragmented sequence without clear grouping. Problems should be organized to show they are distinct manifestations of the underlying root cause (process fragmentation and unclear ownership), making the logic mutually exclusive and collectively exhaustive.

**6. Diagnostic Insight Buried**
The real analytical work—identifying that fragmentation, unclear responsibility, and lack of end-to-end control are the drivers—appears only in "Overall assessment." This diagnosis should be the foundation (or near the apex) of the pyramid, supporting everything else.
