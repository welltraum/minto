Subject: Recommendation to Reduce Composing-Room Costs at TTW

**Recommendation**
TTW should immediately launch a two-part initiative to reduce composing costs and restore competitiveness on simple work: (1) simplify quality-control procedures for low-complexity titles, and (2) conduct a formal methods study to close the 20–50% productivity gap against industry benchmarks. This approach will directly address the uncertainty surrounding excessive costs while delivering measurable savings.

**Supporting Analysis**
**1. Simplify processing for straightforward titles to capture immediate savings.**
- Composition accounts for approximately 40% of hardback costs and 50–55% of paperback costs, making it TTW’s largest production expense.
- Currently, every title—whether a complex reference book or a simple novel—passes through identical quality-control stages.
- Piloting fewer or differently timed checks on selected simple titles could reduce total composing costs by up to 10%, provided quality and customer satisfaction remain unaffected.

**2. Implement a dedicated methods study to resolve the productivity deficit.**
- TTW’s manual composition output trails industry benchmarks by 20–50%, confirming weak productivity and contributing to uncompetitiveness on standard work.
- Modifying the composing method and streamlining workflow will directly address this gap.
- A focused methods study is required to identify specific operational improvements and quantify additional savings beyond the initial 10%.

**3. Validate findings through controlled testing and external benchmarking.**
- Direct comparisons with three peer printers offer useful contextual data, though they cannot independently resolve the cost question.
- Controlled pilot tests and structured benchmarking will determine whether TTW’s costs are genuinely excessive and establish realistic performance targets.
- Despite prior disagreements on cost levels, management has agreed to proceed with investigation and testing.
