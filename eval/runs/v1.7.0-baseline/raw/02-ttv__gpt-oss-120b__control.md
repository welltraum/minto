**Subject:** Reduce TTW composing‑room costs  

---

### Recommendation  
**Simplify the composing process for low‑complexity titles and run a pilot to test reduced quality‑control steps, targeting at least a 10 % cut in composing cost; simultaneously launch a productivity‑improvement programme to close the 20‑50 % gap with industry benchmarks.**  

---

### Why the recommendation will work  

1. **Composition is the biggest cost driver** – it accounts for ~40 % of hard‑back and 50‑55 % of paperback production.  
2. **Productivity is well below peers** – TTW trails comparable printers by 20‑50 %, indicating that costs are excessive rather than simply market‑priced.  
3. **The department is overloaded** – manual composition capacity is constrained, overtime exceeds budget by >50 %, and recent staff turnover has reduced staffing levels, amplifying cost pressure.  
4. **Targeted simplification can deliver savings** – industry experience shows that removing non‑critical checks on inexpensive titles can shave roughly 10 % off total composing cost without harming quality.  

---

### Supporting evidence  

| Evidence | Detail |
|----------|--------|
| **Interviews** | Roy Walter, Brian Thompson, and George Kennedy confirmed that all titles follow the same quality‑control stages and expressed willingness to test process changes. |
| **Benchmark analysis** | TTW’s productivity is 20‑50 % lower than three peer printers, confirming excess cost. |
| **Labor pressure** | Overtime costs >50 % of budget; a union claim is pending, underscoring staffing and capacity issues. |
| **Compensation** | TTW pays below local market rates yet struggles to hire/retain compositors, highlighting systemic capacity constraints. |

---

### Immediate next steps  

1. **Select pilot titles** – choose a representative set of low‑complexity books/novels.  
2. **Redesign workflow** – remove or reschedule non‑essential quality checks for the pilot.  
3. **Measure outcomes** – track cost, turnaround time, and quality against baseline over a two‑month period.  
4. **Parallel methods study** – investigate automation, workflow redesign, and other productivity levers.  
5. **Review & decide** – evaluate pilot results and determine rollout scope.
