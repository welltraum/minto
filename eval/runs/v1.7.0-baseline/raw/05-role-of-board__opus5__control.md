The October reorganization gave divisional managers full authority for day-to-day operations, leaving the Board responsible for policy and planning. Yet the Board continues to spend most of its time on short-term operational problems, as it has for years. If the Board is to take up the role the reorganization intends for it, it must now decide how it should be constituted and how it should work.

Five questions have to be settled:

1. **What should the Board do, and what should the Executive Committee do?** The responsibilities of each body, and the relationship between them, need to be defined before the remaining questions can be answered.

2. **How large should the Board be, and who should sit on it?** Size and membership follow from the work the Board is to do.

3. **How can outside directors be made effective participants?** Outside members contribute the independent judgement that policy and planning require, but only if they are given the information and the standing to use it.

4. **How should directors be selected, and for how long should they serve?** Selection criteria and tenure rules determine whether the Board sustains the composition it sets out to achieve.

5. **How should we move from the present arrangement to the one we want?** Several routes are open, and the choice among them affects how quickly the change takes hold.
