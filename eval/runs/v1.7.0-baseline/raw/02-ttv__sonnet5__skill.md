Subject: Two studies can cut TTW's composing cost by 10%+ — recommend proceeding

Situation: Composition is TTW's largest production cost — roughly 40% of hardback cost and 50-55% of paperback cost — and every title, simple or complex, passes through the same quality-control stages regardless of value.

Complication: TTW is uncompetitive on simple work, and Aylesbury does not yet know whether its composing costs are actually excessive or, if so, why.

Answer: Two targeted studies — simplifying quality control on inexpensive titles, and a methods study of the composing process — should cut composing cost by at least 10%, with further savings possible from closing a 20-50% productivity gap against the benchmark.

Recommendation, in order of expected payoff:

1. **Simplify quality control on inexpensive titles.** Test fewer, differently timed checks on selected jobs and measure the effect on quality and customer response. Expected saving: ~10% of total composing cost.

2. **Run a methods study of the composing process.** Measure why productivity trails the benchmark by 20-50% and identify changes to the composing method that would close the gap. Potential saving: additional, not yet quantified.

3. **Address the capacity shortage feeding current overruns.** The manual composing department is overloaded — below-market pay, difficulty hiring and retaining compositors, two recent departures, chronic lateness, and overtime running 50%+ over budget — so it should be fixed alongside the cost studies rather than treated as a separate problem.

Next step: George Kennedy has agreed to lead the quality-control test and the productivity review; request sign-off to begin both within [timeframe].
