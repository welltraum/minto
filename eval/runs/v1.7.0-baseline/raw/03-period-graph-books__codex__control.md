## Minto Pyramid Audit

**Overall:** The memo identifies real operational problems, but it does not form a clear pyramid. It opens with process detail, postpones its main conclusion, and ends with an unfocused request to “discuss.”

### Primary defects

- **No governing thought.** The document never states the recommended action and its supporting reasons up front. “The process badly needs streamlining” is a diagnosis, not a complete answer to management’s request for corrective proposals.

- **Weak SCQA introduction.** The context explains the transfer of responsibility, but the complication is scattered through the production and issues sections. The question management needs answered is implied rather than explicitly framed: what corrective action should be taken, by whom, and how urgently?

- **Bottom-up structure.** The reader receives production steps, examples, and symptoms before being told the overall assessment. Senior management should see the conclusion first, then the reasons.

- **Mixed levels of abstraction.** “Production,” individual workflow steps, one-book examples, operational symptoms, and organizational conclusions sit alongside one another without a consistent hierarchy.

- **Reasons are not grouped into mutually exclusive categories.** The issues intermingle:
  - fragmented handoffs and repeated entry;
  - inconsistent/incorrect data and calculations;
  - unclear end-to-end ownership;
  - insufficient capacity to redesign while producing the books.
  
  These should be separated into distinct, collectively exhaustive supporting arguments.

- **Evidence is not ordered to prove the conclusion.** The detailed process description and examples are useful evidence, but they should support a higher-level point rather than constitute the main line of argument.

- **The proposed outcome is incomplete.** The memo calls for streamlining but does not specify the decision required: for example, establish single ownership, redesign a common data/calculation process, eliminate duplicate re-entry, and provide dedicated implementation capacity.

- **Closing is too vague.** “Could we discuss how best to proceed?” does not give management a decision, recommendation, or next action to approve.

### What must change

1. Start with a concise SCQA that leads directly to the management question.
2. State one governing thought: the corrective proposal/decision sought.
3. Support it with a small set of parallel, logically ordered reasons—preferably organizational control, process redesign, and implementation capacity.
4. Move workflow detail and examples beneath the relevant reason as proof.
5. End with a specific requested decision and accountable next steps.
