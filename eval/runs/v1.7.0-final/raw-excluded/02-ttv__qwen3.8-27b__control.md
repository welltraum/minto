# TTW Composing-Room Costs

**To:** Engagement Manager  
**From:** Junior Consultant  
**Subject:** Reduce composing-room costs by simplifying checks for inexpensive titles and improving composition methods

## Recommendation

TTW should reduce composing-room costs by (1) piloting simplified quality-control checks for inexpensive titles and (2) carrying out a methods study to improve composition productivity. The pilot could save up to 10% of total composing cost; the methods study could identify further savings because TTW trails the benchmark by 20–50%.

## Key lines supporting the recommendation

### 1. The cost exposure is material

- Composition is the largest production cost, accounting for roughly 40% of hardback cost and 50–55% of paperback cost.
- TTW is uncompetitive on simple work, making simple-work efficiency a priority.

### 2. The current process is not tailored to title complexity

- Every title, whether a complex reference book or a simple novel, passes through essentially the same quality-control stages.
- Inexpensive titles may therefore be subject to checks that are not proportionate to their value or complexity.

### 3. Productivity and capacity indicate further savings are possible

- Measured productivity trails the benchmark by 20–50%.
- The composing department is overloaded, manual composition is especially short of capacity, most jobs are running late, and overtime is more than 50% over budget.

## Recommended actions

1. **Pilot simplified checks for inexpensive titles.** Select representative jobs, remove or retime selected quality-control checks, and measure the effect on quality and customer satisfaction before wider adoption. George Kennedy is willing to help test whether selected stages can be removed or retimed.

2. **Carry out a methods study.** Examine TTW’s composing methods against benchmark practice to identify why measured productivity trails the benchmark and to find further productivity improvements.

3. **Limit the use of external comparisons.** Comparisons with three other printers may provide context, but they should not be treated as decisive evidence that TTW’s costs are excessive.
