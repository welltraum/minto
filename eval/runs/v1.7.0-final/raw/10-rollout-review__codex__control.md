# Fieldbook rollout: extend to North and Islands in Q1—conditionally

## Recommendation

Approve a phased Q1 extension to North and Islands, beginning only after Kestrel has put a repeatable onboarding model and named integration ownership in place. The live rollout demonstrates that Fieldbook can produce attractive returns; the risks are concentrated in known, fixable deployment and integration gaps rather than in the platform itself.

## Why this is the right decision

1. Fieldbook is already improving operating performance and has paid back in the regions that adopted quickly.

2. The barriers to scaling are specific and addressable before launch.

3. Without program-level controls, North and Islands are likely to repeat the slower, break-even rollout pattern seen in South and West.

---

# 1. Fieldbook’s economics support expansion when adoption is rapid

## The live regions show material operational and financial gains

- Completed jobs per technician per day increased from 4.6 to 5.3.
- Travel time between jobs fell 18%; fuel cost per job fell 9%.
- Overtime spending fell 12% quarter over quarter.
- Same-day emergency jobs can now be assigned automatically in a median four minutes.
- Central and East recovered running costs within the second quarter.

## Slower adoption removes those gains

- South needed 11 weeks to exceed 80% of work orders in Fieldbook; West remains at 71% after nine weeks.
- South and West are approximately break-even because adoption was delayed.
- Improvised shadowing consumed an estimated 380 technician-hours of route time.
- The billing workaround required a temporary clerk costing £8,400 over two quarters.

## The extension is therefore commercially attractive only if it follows the fast-adoption model

- North and Islands will add £13,500 per month in licences.
- Replicating Central and East’s adoption pace should make the extension favourable.
- Repeating West’s pattern would leave the new regions underwater for at least two quarters.

---

# 2. Kestrel should remove the known readiness gaps before go-live

## Replace one-off classroom training with complete, supported onboarding

- Account activation is 412 of 486 technicians; missed training is the main cause of weak uptake.
- In West, 34 of 118 technicians attended no session.
- Login and password issues account for 44% of support tickets and are concentrated among technicians who missed training.
- Dispatchers achieved immediate adoption because the old process was retired on day one and they had no alternative.

**Required condition:** provide multiple training sessions, a mandatory activation window, and scheduled catch-up sessions for absences and new hires. Use the weekly support webinar as reinforcement, not as the primary onboarding channel.

## Fix the two integration failures with accountable ownership

- Missing asset-register history causes technicians to work without warranty and service context; 9% of audited jobs included parts replaced within warranty in the prior year.
- Completion-code differences force billing to re-key roughly 300 records each week and create 2–4% reporting-to-invoice discrepancies.
- The vendor has a configurable code mapping that takes about a day to establish; supported connectors exist for both systems.

**Required condition:** appoint one Kestrel integration owner, evaluate the vendor connectors, implement the completion-code mapping before launch, and commit a dated plan for the asset-history feed.

## Reduce avoidable support workload

- Exact-postcode search leads to duplicate customer records; support merges about 70 per week.
- Duplicate records split job histories until they are merged.

**Required condition:** obtain a vendor remedy or operating workaround for customer search before the second regional launch, with duplicate creation tracked weekly.

---

# 3. Deliver the extension as a controlled two-region program

## Launch North first, then release Islands only after defined gates are met

North should be the initial Q1 launch. Islands should follow only when North demonstrates that the revised model is working.

| Gate | Measure |
|---|---|
| Onboarding | All active technicians have attended training and activated accounts before unsupported operation |
| Adoption | At least 80% of work orders created and closed in Fieldbook within six weeks |
| Operations | No unresolved asset-history or completion-code workaround preventing normal field or billing activity |
| Support | Login-related tickets decline after the initial launch period; duplicate records are controlled |
| Economics | Route-time and overtime improvements are visible without material shadowing or manual billing cost |

## Establish one program-level feedback loop

- Review launch metrics weekly for the first six weeks, then monthly.
- Give the program office authority to convert recurring field observations into changes across both regions.
- Track a single issue register covering onboarding completion, adoption, support demand, integrations, data quality, and local workarounds.
- Escalate unresolved gate failures to the COO before authorising the Islands launch.

## Assign clear ownership

- **Program office:** launch governance, gates, and cross-region learning.
- **Operations:** technician attendance, adoption, and route-performance measures.
- **IT / named integration owner:** asset-history feed and completion-code mapping.
- **Support desk:** onboarding reinforcement, ticket trends, and duplicate-record reporting.
- **Finance:** benefit tracking and confirmation that the extension remains on its expected payback path.
