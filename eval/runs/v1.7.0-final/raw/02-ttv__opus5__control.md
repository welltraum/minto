Subject: Reducing TTW composing-room costs

**TTW can cut composing costs by an estimated 10% or more, and should authorise two tests to confirm it.** Composition is the largest single production cost, and two independent levers are available: simplifying the process for inexpensive titles, and raising productivity by changing the composing method. Management disagrees about whether costs are high, but all parties are willing to investigate.

## 1. Simplify quality control on inexpensive titles

Every title passes through essentially the same quality-control stages, whether it is a complex reference book or a simple novel. TTW is uncompetitive on simple work, which suggests the stages are heavier than cheap titles require.

*Recommended test:* run selected jobs with fewer or differently timed checks and measure the effect on quality and on customers. George Kennedy has agreed to run this test. Potential saving: up to 10% of total composing cost.

## 2. Change the composing method to raise productivity

Industry comparisons put TTW 20–50% behind the benchmark on measured productivity.

*Recommended test:* a separate methods study to identify why productivity trails and which changes would close the gap.

## 3. Why the tests are necessary now

The department is overloaded, and manual composition is especially short of capacity. TTW pays below local market rates, cannot reliably hire or retain compositors, has just lost two employees, and now runs most jobs late. Overtime is more than 50% over budget while the union presses a new claim. Cost relief will not come from working the existing process harder.
