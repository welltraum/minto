The October reorganization gave divisional managers full authority for day-to-day operations, leaving the Board responsible for policy and planning. In practice, however, the Board has for years spent most of its time on short-term operational problems. It has not yet reshaped itself for the role it now holds.

Before the Board can concentrate on policy and planning, it must settle five questions about its own structure and working methods:

1. What should the Board's composition and optimum size be?
2. What are the respective roles and responsibilities of the Board and the Executive Committee, and how should the two relate to each other?
3. How can an outside director be made an effective participant?
4. What principles should govern the selection of directors and the length of their tenure?
5. By what route should the Board move from the present arrangement to the one it decides it wants?
