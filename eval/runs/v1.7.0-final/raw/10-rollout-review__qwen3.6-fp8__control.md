**RECOMMENDATION**

Extend Fieldbook to North and Islands in Q1, conditional on restructuring the onboarding model and executing integration fixes prior to launch.

**1. The business case for expansion is validated and economically favorable.**

*   Operational efficiency gains are significant and consistent across live regions.
    *   Completed jobs per technician rose from 4.6 to 5.3, and average travel time between jobs fell 18%.
    *   Same-day emergency insertion is now automatic, reducing median assignment time to 4 minutes.
*   Financial returns confirm payback and positive unit economics.
    *   Overtime spending declined 12% quarter-over-quarter, and fuel cost per job dropped 9%.
    *   Central and East reached payback within the second quarter; South and West are at break-even.
    *   Extension adds £13,500 monthly in licences and requires winter travel for classroom training, but economics remain favourable if adoption velocity matches Central and East.

**2. Adoption lags are driven by training logistics, not platform capability, and can be corrected.**

*   High adoption correlates directly with comprehensive training coverage.
    *   Central and East achieved full adoption within six weeks; all 41 dispatchers reached 100% usage immediately.
    *   South and West lagged because the two-day classroom course was scheduled only once per region.
    *   In West, 34 of 118 technicians never attended a session, forcing reliance on shadowing that diverted experienced staff from routes.
*   Support volume confirms training gaps rather than product defects.
    *   44% of tickets are password resets concentrated among technicians who missed classroom sessions.
    *   The support desk is effectively performing training functions at ticket costs, indicating a need for proactive, continuous onboarding.
*   New regions require a restructured training approach to avoid repeating the West pattern.
    *   A single classroom event cannot support rolling hires; a hybrid model with weekly webinars and immediate access is required.
    *   Early warnings from Central were not converted into program-level fixes; new regions must implement the revised training model before go-live.

**3. Integration gaps create measurable friction but have clear, low-effort resolutions.**

*   Missing asset register feed compromises service quality and increases waste.
    *   Technicians lack equipment history, leading to unnecessary part replacements in 9% of audited jobs.
    *   Back-office calls increase as technicians compensate for missing data.
*   Job-completion code mismatches disrupt billing and reporting accuracy.
    *   The billing team manually re-keys approximately 300 records weekly, introducing transcription errors.
    *   Reporting and invoicing systems disagree on completed-job counts by 2–4% weekly.
*   Vendor solutions are ready and require only internal execution.
    *   Connectors for the asset register and invoicing system exist in the vendor catalogue.
    *   IT assesses both integrations as straightforward; the barrier is lack of scope and ownership, not technical complexity.
    *   A configurable mapping table for codes can be deployed in roughly one day.
    *   Pre-launch actions must assign owners and scope these integrations to protect data integrity and billing efficiency.
