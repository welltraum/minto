Subject: Reduce TTW composing costs through process simplification and a methods study

TTW should begin a targeted cost-reduction programme in the composing room: test a simplified quality-control process for low-cost titles and commission a methods study to improve productivity.

This is warranted because TTW applies essentially the same checking process to simple novels and complex reference works, while its productivity trails industry benchmarks by 20–50%. Simplifying the process for selected inexpensive titles could save up to 10% of total composing cost without compromising required quality or customer service.

The programme should have two workstreams:

1. Test streamlined controls on selected simple titles. George Kennedy is willing to lead trials that remove or retime selected quality checks, measuring effects on errors, quality, delivery, and customer response.

2. Conduct a methods study of composing practices. The study should identify the sources of TTW’s productivity gap and recommend changes to the composing method.

This work is urgent. The department is overloaded, manual composition lacks capacity, two employees have recently left, most jobs are late, and overtime is more than 50% above budget. Low local pay, hiring and retention difficulties, and a pending union claim increase the need to improve productivity rather than rely on additional labour.

Omitted or demoted: interviews and names not relevant to implementation; the proposed comparison with three other printers, since it is unlikely to resolve TTW’s cost issue; and the unresolved debate over whether costs are high, since managers agree to investigate and the operational evidence supports action.
