## Audit — "Period Graph Books"

**Reader's question:** management asked for corrective proposals, so the question is *"What should we do to stop the errors in the graph books?"* The answer must therefore consist of **actions** — the changes to be made, who makes them, what it costs. The memo supplies a process description and a diagnosis of causes instead, and never reaches an action.

**Legend:** `▲` top/answer · `●` first-level group · `○` support · `⚠` violation · `↑` conclusion buried too low · `⇄` wrong order type · `⊗` overlap · `⊕` gap · `✂` cut or move to appendix

### Findings

| # | Location | Violation | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Whole document | **No answer exists anywhere.** The reader asked what to change; the document delivers a description and a diagnosis. Even the closing paragraph stops at "needs streamlining" | Derive the actions from the causes already established and put them on the first level: single end-to-end ownership of the data, one entry of each figure, one shared calculation base for the common points |
| 2 | Subject line / opening | Top is a topic label, and the controlling idea ("most problems arise from a fragmented process") sits in the final paragraph | Promote the answer to the subject line and first sentence; the fragmentation finding becomes its support |
| 3 | "Production" as a first-level group | Wrong kind: current-state mechanics standing where the reader's question demands changes | Demote under the change each step justifies, or move the whole walkthrough to an appendix |
| 4 | "Issues" | Mixed kinds — causes and symptoms in one group with no intermediate conclusion linking them | Separate: symptoms are evidence, the cause is the intermediate conclusion, the action is what follows from it |
| 5 | "For one book…" paragraph | Overlap: re-tells the Production steps at finer grain as though it were a distinct issue | Merge into the fragmentation support, or cut |
| 6 | Closing paragraph, last sentence | Buried ask: the resource request appears as an aside, unquantified | Raise to a first-level element, with the specific resource and the period it is needed for |
| 7 | Opening paragraph | Intro has a Situation but no Complication and no Question; "this memo describes issues" is a purpose statement | Replace with the complication — errors are reaching senior management — so the question the memo answers is visible before the support begins |

Two further findings are omitted: the missing scale evidence under every symptom (`⊕`), and the unexplained order of the four Issues.

### The three fixes that buy the most

1. **State the actions.** Replace "the process badly needs streamlining" with the two or three specific changes the memo's own evidence supports, each with an owner and a date.
2. **Lift the fragmentation conclusion to the top and put the process description beneath it** — the reader should meet the finding first and the mechanics only as proof.
3. **Make the resource request explicit and first-level**, in place of "could we discuss how best to proceed".
