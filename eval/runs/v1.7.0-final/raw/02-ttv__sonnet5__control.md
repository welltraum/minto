Subject: Reducing TTW's composing costs

**We recommend TTW pursue two actions to reduce composing costs, which we estimate could save at least 10% of total composing cost, with more potentially available from a productivity study.**

1. **Simplify the quality-control process for simple titles.** Every title—complex reference book or simple novel—currently passes through the same checking stages. We recommend testing fewer or differently timed checks on selected jobs and measuring the effect on quality and customer satisfaction. Estimated saving: 10% of total composing cost.

2. **Investigate productivity through a formal methods study.** TTW's measured productivity trails the industry benchmark by 20-50%. George Kennedy has agreed to test which stages can be removed and why productivity lags. This study should identify the specific causes and quantify further savings.

**Supporting context**

Composition is TTW's largest production cost (roughly 40% of hardback cost, 50-55% of paperback), and industry comparisons show TTW is uncompetitive, particularly on simple work. The department is also under capacity pressure: manual composition is short-staffed, pay is below local market rates, two compositors have recently left, most jobs are running late, and overtime is over 50% over budget. These pressures make the recommended actions more urgent but are not themselves solutions—they explain why action is needed now.
