Audit findings:

- The opening states the topic and purpose rather than providing a key-line conclusion; it does not tell the reader what to believe, decide, or do.
- It announces the document’s mechanical organization (“six sections”) instead of presenting the main supporting ideas.
- The headings are nominal labels, not complete thought-bearing statements, so they do not communicate the point of each section.
- The six headings are presented as a flat list with no visible grouping or hierarchy; the setout does not show how the sections support one another or the opening claim.
- The items mix different kinds of content—background, principles, definition, organization, benefits/results, and conditions—so the list lacks a clear, single organizing principle and does not visibly satisfy MECE.
- The stated purpose (“how project teams identify and deliver significant profit improvements”) is not visibly aligned with all listed sections; “Distinctive benefits and results” and “Conditions required for success” are not parts of the stated “how.”
- The opening asserts “significant profit improvements” without any visible supporting structure in the available text.
- The scope terms shift among “project teams,” “project-team approach,” “project work,” and “program,” leaving the subject boundaries unclear.
