**Audit findings**

1. **No summary/key line.** The passage lists six section titles but never states the point they collectively make (Minto's requirement that a set of grouped ideas be capped by a single summarizing statement). The reader is told *what* is covered, not *why* it matters or what conclusion the memo is driving toward.

2. **Headings are not parallel in kind.** They mix categories that answer different questions:
   - "Background," "Definition of project work," "Organization of the program" — descriptive/expository (what is it, how does it work)
   - "Principles of the project-team approach" — conceptual
   - "Distinctive benefits and results" — evaluative (why it's good)
   - "Conditions required for success" — conditional/prescriptive

   Minto's grouping test requires items in a list to be the "same kind of thing" (nouns naming a single class of idea). Here the list fails that test — it reads as a table of contents assembled by topic area rather than a set of ideas that share a common logical relationship (e.g., all reasons, all steps, all benefits).

3. **No stated logical order.** Six headings are presented with no visible rationale for sequence — not chronological, not order-of-importance, not deductive. Minto requires that any grouped set follow one of the three groupings (time, structure, or degree) and that the order be evident from the wording. As written, nothing signals why "Background" precedes "Principles" or why "Benefits" comes before "Conditions for success" rather than after.

4. **Headings don't reveal the argument.** Each title names a topic ("Background," "Definition," "Organization") rather than an assertion. Minto's guidance is that section headings, like the key line, should let the reader anticipate the point being made in each section — not merely the subject matter. As given, none of the six tells the reader what will be *claimed* about that topic.

5. **Cannot verify count/order against a governing thought.** Because no introductory key sentence or governing idea is given (only "It is organized in six sections"), there is no way to confirm the six headings are complete, mutually exclusive, or correctly sequenced relative to the memo's actual point. This is flagged as missing rather than assumed.

No claims are made about the body content, since it is not available.
