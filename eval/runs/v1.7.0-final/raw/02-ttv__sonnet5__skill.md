Subject: TTW composing costs — recommended next steps

Composition drives 40% of hardback and 50-55% of paperback cost, and TTW is uncompetitive on simple work — but we still don't know whether that cost is actually excessive, because every title, simple or complex, runs through the same quality-control stages regardless of need. Two weeks of fact-finding point to a clear next step: run two parallel tests to find out where the money can come out — simplifying quality control on simple titles, and improving compositor productivity.

1. Test whether quality control can be simplified on simple titles
   - Select a sample of straightforward jobs and reduce or retime the checks they go through
   - Measure the effect on quality and customer acceptance before rolling out
   - Potential saving: up to 10% of total composing cost

2. Test whether a different composing method raises productivity
   - Commission a methods study on manual composition, the tightest capacity constraint
   - TTW currently trails the productivity benchmark by 20-50%; the study should identify where that gap can close
   - George Kennedy has agreed to run this test

What I need now: sign-off to launch both tests, with Kennedy leading the productivity study.
