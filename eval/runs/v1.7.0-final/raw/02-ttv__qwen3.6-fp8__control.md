**Subject: Recommendations to Reduce TTW Composing Costs**

**Main Conclusion**
To reduce composing costs and restore market competitiveness, TTW should immediately pilot a simplified quality-control process for low-complexity titles and commission a formal methods study to address systemic productivity gaps.

**Supporting Arguments & Evidence**
1. **Simplifying quality control for straightforward titles will yield immediate, measurable savings.**
   - Currently, every title—regardless of complexity—passes through identical quality-assurance stages.
   - Piloting fewer or differently timed checks on selected jobs is projected to capture up to 10% of total composing costs.
   - Trials must track both quality metrics and customer feedback to confirm service standards remain intact.

2. **A dedicated methods study will resolve the department’s critical productivity deficit.**
   - TTW’s composing productivity trails industry benchmarks by 20–50%, making the firm uncompetitive on standard work.
   - The department is severely overloaded: manual composition is at capacity, most jobs run late, and overtime exceeds budget by over 50%.
   - Although managers disagree on whether current costs are excessive, all have committed to investigating root causes and testing solutions.

**Material Omitted or Demoted**
- Names of interviewed personnel (Roy Walter, Brian Thompson, George Kennedy) and general commentary on local stakeholder interest, as they do not advance the strategic recommendation.
- Details regarding the union’s new wage claim and specific recruitment/retention difficulties, which are operational constraints rather than direct levers for the proposed cost-reduction strategy.
- Benchmarking against three external printers, as preliminary assessment indicates this comparison will not resolve the core productivity question.
- Repetitive statements regarding composition’s share of total hardback/paperback costs, which were already confirmed in prior engagement briefings.
