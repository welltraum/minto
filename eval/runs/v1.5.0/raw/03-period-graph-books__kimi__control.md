## Audit: "Period Graph Books" against the Minto Pyramid Principle

### 1. There is no governing thought

The subject line names a topic ("Period Graph Books") but the memo never states a governing idea. A Minto document must open with a single answer to the reader's question — here, something like "the graph-book process must be redesigned and resourced separately." Instead, the memo dribbles out chronology ("Since Period 5..."), process steps, and issues, and only gestures at a conclusion ("Could we discuss how best to proceed?"). That closing question is not a governing thought; it is an abdication of one. The reader asked for corrective proposals and gets none.

**What must change:** Lead with the answer — a one- or two-sentence governing thought stating that the process is structurally fragmented, that errors are its inevitable product, and that the remedy is a dedicated streamlining effort. Everything below should exist only to support that assertion.

### 2. The introduction does not follow Situation–Complication–Question–Answer

The opening mixes situation ("we took over four books") with process narration before any complication is established. Worse, the actual context the reader supplied — management knows errors exist and asked for proposals — is never stated. The S-C-Q-A introduction should build the reader's known situation, introduce the complication (errors and inconsistencies post-transfer), pose the reader's question (how do we fix it?), and answer it. This memo has no Q and no A.

### 3. The "Production" section is narrative, not logic

Steps 1–4 describe how the books are made, in chronological order. In a pyramid, narrative process description is raw material, not an argument. It belongs in an appendix or a compressed sentence of evidence. As written, the reader must do the analysis the writer should have done: infer that a four-step, multi-department, triple-entry chain is inherently error-prone.

**What must change:** Convert the narrative into a deductive or inductive argument: e.g., "the process guarantees errors because (a) the same data are entered up to three times, (b) no single owner controls data end to end, (c) prior-period and cross-period consistency checks are structurally impossible." Each production detail then appears once, as support under the point it proves.

### 4. The "Issues" section is an unstructured list, not a grouping

The issues named — inconsistent data between periods and regions, incorrect variable-cost calculations, unexplained changes to previously correct points, prior-period re-entry — are dumped in one sentence without grouping, ordering, or MECE logic. Under Minto, items at one level must be of the same kind, mutually exclusive, collectively exhaustive, and ordered by importance. Here they are a mixed bag: some are data-integrity symptoms, some are calculation defects, some are process artifacts.

**What must change:** Group the issues under the few causes that generate them (likely: redundant manual entry, unclear ownership, lack of end-to-end control), and state each group with a summary assertion, not a bare list.

### 5. Two competing lines of reasoning are interleaved

The memo contains two distinct arguments that are never separated:

- **Diagnostic:** errors come from a fragmented, ownerless process (problem → cause).
- **Action:** the current team cannot both run production and fix the process (constraint → need for separate resources).

The "Overall assessment" lumps both together in four sentences. Under Minto these are different pyramid branches and should be explicit, each with its own key-line point — especially the second, because it is the real request being made of management and it currently hides at the bottom of the document.

### 6. The key line is missing

A pyramid's key line — the 2–4 parallel points that support the governing thought — does not exist. A compliant structure would be roughly:

1. Errors are systemic, not incidental (evidence: the grouped issues).
2. The cause is structural: fragmented, multiply-entered, unowned data flow (evidence: the production chain).
3. Therefore the process needs redesign, and redesign requires a separate effort beyond the current team's capacity.

Nothing in the current memo is organized along these lines.

### Summary of defects

- No governing thought; no answer to the reader's stated request.
- No S-C-Q-A introduction; background and process narration substitute for one.
- Chronological description presented where argument is required.
- Issues listed flat, without MECE grouping or summary assertions.
- Diagnosis and recommendation conflated; the actual ask (separate resources for redesign) buried at the end.
- No key-line structure tying the parts to a single conclusion.

The diagnosis: this is a chronologically-organized report, not a pyramid. The fix is not better prose but restructuring — state the answer first, then support it with grouped, mutually reinforcing lines of argument, pushing process detail down to its proper evidentiary level.
