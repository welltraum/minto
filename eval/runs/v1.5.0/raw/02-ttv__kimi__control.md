**To:** Engagement Manager
**From:** Junior Consultant
**Subject:** TTW — Composing-Room Costs Can Probably Be Reduced

## Governing thought

TTW's composing costs are very likely excessive, and they can probably be reduced in two ways: by simplifying the production process for inexpensive titles, and by raising composing-room productivity. I recommend we pursue both through small, controlled tests.

## Key line

**1. TTW applies a full process to every title, however simple — so simplifying the process for inexpensive titles is the most direct way to cut cost.**
Every title, from a complex reference book to a simple novel, passes through essentially the same quality-control stages. This is a major reason TTW is uncompetitive on simple work. I recommend testing selected simple jobs with fewer, or differently timed, checks, and measuring the effect on quality and on customers. The potential saving could reach 10% of total composing cost. George Kennedy has agreed to run this test.

**2. Measured productivity trails the industry benchmark by 20–50% — so changing the composing method offers a second, larger opportunity.**
Industry comparisons indicate weak productivity and high cost at TTW, and the symptoms on the floor are consistent with that: the department is overloaded, manual composition is critically short of capacity, TTW pays below local market rates and cannot hire or retain compositors (two have just left), most jobs now run late, and overtime is more than 50% over budget. A dedicated methods study should identify where the 20–50% gap comes from and what a changed composing method could recover.

**3. Both investigations are feasible now.**
Management is willing to investigate, and a named owner (Kennedy) is already committed to the simplification test.
