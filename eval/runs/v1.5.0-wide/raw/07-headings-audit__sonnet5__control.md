## Audit Findings

**1. Headings are topic labels, not key-line points.**
Minto's structure requires each heading to state the substance of what the reader will learn (a sentence-level point), not a category name. All six entries here — "Background," "Principles of the project-team approach," "Definition of project work," "Organization of the program," "Distinctive benefits and results," "Conditions required for success" — are noun-phrase labels. None tells the reader what is actually said about the topic. This means the "setout" cannot yet be checked for logical grouping or MECE compliance, since it doesn't disclose the actual claims.

**2. No governing thought / summary sentence precedes the list.**
The opening states only the subject of the memo ("how project teams identify and deliver significant profit improvements") and that it is organized in six sections. It does not give the single controlling idea the sections are meant to support. Per the Pyramid Principle, the introduction should lead the reader to anticipate the key line before it is listed, and the list should read as the "children" answering "why" or "how" of that governing thought. Here the six items are simply enumerated with no stated logical relationship to each other or to a top point.

**3. The six items are not visibly grouped by type of logic.**
A proper key line groups points either by structural/deductive logic ("what," "so what," "why") or by like-with-like (e.g., all describing steps, all describing benefits). As presented, the sequence mixes categories that appear to differ in kind: background/definition/principles look descriptive; organization looks procedural; benefits/conditions look evaluative. Whether this mixing is a real MECE violation cannot be confirmed without the body, but the *headings alone* give no indication of how they cohere — that grouping logic is not surfaced.

**4. Parallel construction is inconsistent.**
"Background," "Definition of project work," "Organization of the program" are noun phrases; "Distinctive benefits and results" and "Conditions required for success" shift register slightly (evaluative adjectives, "required for"). This inconsistency is a minor symptom of the larger problem (#1): because the headings aren't summary sentences, there's nothing to enforce parallel grammatical or logical form across them.

**No claim is made here about what the six sections actually argue** — the body is unavailable, and the underlying logic could be sound. The issue is that the visible opening and setout do not yet demonstrate that logic; they present a table of contents, not a Minto key line.
