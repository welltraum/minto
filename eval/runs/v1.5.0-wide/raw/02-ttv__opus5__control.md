Subject: TTW — reducing composing-room costs

**TTW should begin two tests now: strip quality-control stages from simple titles, and run a methods study on manual composition. Together these address a cost base where TTW trails comparable printers by 20–50%, and the first alone could save up to 10% of total composing cost. Line management is willing to proceed.**

**1. Simplify the process for inexpensive titles — potential saving up to 10% of composing cost.**

- Every title passes through essentially the same quality-control stages, whether it is a complex reference work or a simple novel.
- The test: select a sample of simple jobs, remove or re-time specific checks, and measure the effect on defect rates and on customer acceptance.
- George Kennedy has agreed to run this test.

**2. Raise productivity by changing the composing method — size not yet quantified.**

- Industry comparison places TTW 20–50% below benchmark productivity, so the opportunity is likely larger than the process saving, but it cannot be sized without a methods study.
- Manual composition is the constraint: the department is overloaded, most jobs now run late, and overtime is more than 50% over budget.
- The capacity shortfall is partly a labour problem. TTW pays below local market rates, cannot hire or retain compositors, and has just lost two. The union is pressing a new claim. A methods study should therefore examine pay and manning alongside technique, since a method change that assumes current staffing will not hold.

**3. Neither test depends on first settling whether TTW's costs are "excessive."**

- Managers in Aylesbury disagree on that question; Roy Walter, Brian Thompson, and George Kennedy all want the causes investigated regardless.
- A cost comparison with three other printers would give context, but differences in title mix and equipment mean it is unlikely to resolve the disagreement. Recommend it only as background, not as a precondition for the tests.

**Next step:** confirm Kennedy's test sample and scope the methods study, including its treatment of pay and manning.
