Since the reorganization gave divisional managers full authority for day-to-day operations, the Board is now free to focus exclusively on policy and planning—but doing so effectively requires resolving several open questions about how the Board itself should be structured and run. This memorandum proposes answers to four questions:

1. What size and composition would best equip the Board for its policy role?
2. How should work be divided between the Board and the Executive Committee, and how should the two relate?
3. How can outside directors be made effective participants?
4. What principles should govern the selection and tenure of directors?

It also suggests how the Board might move from its present arrangement with the Executive Committee to the one proposed.
