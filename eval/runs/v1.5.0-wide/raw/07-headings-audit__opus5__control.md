## Audit — Fixture 07

**Verdict:** The opening fails at the pyramid's top. It announces a topic and a table of contents, but never states a governing thought.

### 1. No answer at the apex
"This memorandum describes how project teams identify and deliver significant profit improvements" names a *subject*, not a *point*. The reader is told what the document is about, not what it concludes. A Minto opening must assert the single message the six sections collectively support; nothing here can be argued with, so nothing here is a thesis.

### 2. No question, therefore no reason to read
There is no Situation–Complication–Question sequence. Because no question is raised, the six sections answer nothing in particular, and the reader has no basis for judging whether they are the *right* six.

### 3. Headings are category labels, not messages
All six are noun phrases — Background, Principles, Definition, Organization, Benefits, Conditions. Each names a container and leaves the contents unknown. Read end to end, the setout communicates zero substance about the project-team approach: it would sit equally well atop a memorandum arguing for the approach or against it.

### 4. The group is not a logical grouping
The six items are not the parts of any one thing. They mix:
- prefatory material (Background),
- description of the method (Principles, Definition, Organization),
- assessment of the method (Benefits),
- prerequisites for the method (Conditions).

These belong to different levels of abstraction and answer different implied questions. No deductive chain, and no discernible inductive order — not time, not structure, not degree of importance. A reader cannot tell why the list stops at six or why this sequence.

### 5. "Background" should not be a section
Background is introduction. Placing it as the first of six co-equal points implies it is one of the supporting arguments, which it is not. This also inflates the count.

### 6. Six items signals unsummarized thinking
Six is above the point at which a grouping should have been summarized into higher-level ideas. The mixing noted in §4 is the cause: at least two tiers have been flattened into one list. Descriptive sections likely belong under a single point about *what the approach is*; Benefits and Conditions belong to a separate point about *whether to adopt it*.

### 7. Parallelism is inconsistent
"Principles of…", "Definition of…", "Organization of…" are of-phrases; "Background" is a bare noun; "Distinctive benefits and results" carries an adjective and a doublet; "Conditions required for success" is a participial construction. Grammatical form varies without corresponding to any difference in role.

### Not assessed
The body is unavailable. Whether the sections are MECE in content, whether each supports a common point, and whether the argument holds cannot be judged from the setout alone.
