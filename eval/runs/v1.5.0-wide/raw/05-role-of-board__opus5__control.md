Now that divisional managers hold full authority for day-to-day operations, the Board's proper work is policy and planning. In practice, however, the Board continues to spend most of its time on short-term operational problems, as it has for years. It should redefine its role and reorganize itself to fill that role.

Doing so requires the Board to settle five questions:

1. What should the Board's role be, and what should the Executive Committee's be, given that operations now rest with divisional managers?
2. What size and composition does that role imply?
3. How can outside directors be made effective participants rather than nominal ones?
4. On what principles should directors be selected, and for how long should they serve?
5. How should the Board move from the present arrangement to the intended one?
