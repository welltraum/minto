**Subject:** Pilot to Reduce Composing‑Room Costs  

**Situation** – TT TW’s composing room accounts for roughly 40 % of hard‑back and 50‑55 % of paperback production cost. Industry benchmarks show TT TW’s productivity is 20‑50 % below peers, and the department is already overloaded, running overtime at more than 50 % over budget.  

**Complication** – Management does not know whether the composing cost is truly excessive, and there is disagreement about the root causes. Without concrete evidence, any broad cost‑cutting initiative risks disrupting quality or missing the real drivers of the gap.  

**Question** – How can TT TW determine whether simplifying the composing process and improving productivity will deliver meaningful cost savings?  

**Answer** – Run a focused pilot on a sample of inexpensive titles to (1) test a streamlined quality‑control sequence and (2) measure productivity gains from alternative composing methods; use the results, together with a benchmark comparison to three peer printers, to decide on wider implementation.  

---

### Why the pilot is needed  

1. **High cost share & weak productivity** – Composing is the largest cost driver and TT TW trails industry benchmarks by 20‑50 %. A data‑driven test will reveal how much cost can be reclaimed.  
2. **Capacity overload** – Current overtime exceeds budget by >50 % and recent staff turnover has reduced capacity, indicating that any process change must be validated against real‑world workload.  
3. **Uncertainty about excess** – Managers disagree on whether costs are truly high; the pilot provides the evidence needed to reach consensus and justify further investment.  

### What the pilot will do  

- **Select a set of simple titles** (e.g., low‑complexity novels) and run two variants: the existing full quality‑control chain versus a reduced chain with fewer or re‑timed checks.  
- **Measure**: (a) total composing cost per title, (b) productivity (pages per hour), and (c) quality outcomes (defect rate, customer feedback).  
- **Benchmark**: Compare pilot results with data from three comparable printers to contextualise any gains.  

### Expected outcome  

- If the streamlined process cuts composing cost by ≈10 % of the total composing expense and improves productivity without degrading quality, TT TW can roll the change across the portfolio.  
- The benchmark comparison will confirm whether TT TW’s cost structure remains uncompetitive after the pilot, guiding any further strategic actions.  

---

**What was omitted** – Detailed accounts of individual staff turnover, union claim specifics, and the broader discussion of manager disagreement were excluded because they do not directly support the recommendation to test and measure process changes.
