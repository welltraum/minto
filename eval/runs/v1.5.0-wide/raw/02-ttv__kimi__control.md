# TTW composing costs: reduce them by simplifying the process and raising productivity

**To:**
**From:**
**Subject: TTW composing costs**

Composition is TTW's largest production cost — roughly 40% of hardback cost and 50–55% of paperback cost — and the evidence gathered over the past two weeks suggests it can be reduced. TTW should (1) simplify the composing process for inexpensive titles and (2) conduct a methods study to raise productivity. Together these could cut total composing cost by 10% or more.

## 1. TTW's composing costs appear excessive and deserve investigation

TTW is uncompetitive on simple work, and industry comparisons suggest weak productivity: measured output trails the benchmark by 20–50%. Whether costs are truly excessive is not yet proven, but the size of the gap and the share of total cost at stake justify acting now rather than debating the point further.

## 2. Simplify the process for inexpensive titles

Every title, whether a complex reference book or a simple novel, passes through essentially the same quality-control stages. Simple titles almost certainly do not need them all.

TTW should test selected jobs, removing or re-timing some checks, and measure the effect on quality and on customers. The potential saving is up to 10% of total composing cost. George Kennedy has agreed to run the test, including measuring why productivity trails the benchmark.

## 3. Conduct a separate methods study to raise productivity

Because TTW trails the productivity benchmark by 20–50%, a methods study of the composing method itself could identify further improvements beyond the process simplification above.
