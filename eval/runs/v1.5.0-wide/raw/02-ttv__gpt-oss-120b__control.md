**Subject:** Composing‑Room Cost Reduction – Recommendation & Supporting Evidence  

---

## Recommendation  
**Simplify the composing process for low‑complexity titles and launch a focused productivity‑improvement study.**  
*If TTW pilots a streamlined workflow on selected simple books and concurrently investigates alternative composing methods, it can cut composing costs by up to 10 % on those titles and capture additional savings from the broader productivity gap (20‑50 % below industry benchmarks).*

---

## Why the Recommendation Is Needed  

| Issue | Evidence |
|-------|----------|
| **Composing dominates production cost** | ≈ 40 % of hard‑back cost; 50‑55 % of paperback cost. |
| **TTW is uncompetitive on simple work** | Industry cost‑per‑title for simple books is lower; TTW’s total cost is higher despite paying below local market rates. |
| **Productivity lags the benchmark** | Measured output is 20‑50 % below that of three comparable printers. |
| **Capacity strain limits current performance** | Manual composition is overloaded; overtime exceeds budget by > 50 %; recent loss of two compositors has increased lateness. |

These facts show that the composing function is both a cost driver and a bottleneck. Reducing the number of quality‑control stages for low‑complexity titles directly attacks the cost component, while a methods study addresses the broader productivity gap.

---

## Supporting Arguments  

1. **Targeted simplification can deliver immediate savings**  
   *Pilot test on a sample of inexpensive titles.*  
   - Reduce or re‑time quality‑control checks (currently identical for all titles).  
   - Expected cost reduction: **≈ 10 % of total composing cost** for the pilot set, based on industry “simple‑work” benchmarks.  

2. **A methods study can uncover further efficiency gains**  
   - Map current workflow, identify non‑value‑adding steps, and evaluate alternative composing technologies (e.g., semi‑automated layout).  
   - Potential to close the 20‑50 % productivity gap identified against peers.  

3. **Benchmarking provides context and validation**  
   - Compare TTW’s process and cost structure with three peer printers (already identified).  
   - Use findings to set realistic targets and justify any process changes to senior management.  

4. **Implementation is feasible despite staffing constraints**  
   - Pilot uses existing capacity for simple titles; overtime can be re‑allocated from low‑value checks.  
   - Early wins will improve morale and may aid recruitment/retention by demonstrating a commitment to efficiency.
