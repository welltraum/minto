**Audit Findings**

1. **Missing Key Message (Answer) in the Introduction**  
   – The opening sentence states only the purpose of the memorandum (“describes how project teams identify and deliver significant profit improvements”) but does not present the *conclusion* or *recommendation* that the reader should take away. According to the Minto Pyramid Principle, the introduction must begin with the *answer* (the main point or decision) before describing the supporting structure.

2. **Structure Statement Not Aligned with the Pyramid**  
   – The sentence “It is organized in six sections:” merely lists the document’s layout. In a proper Minto opening, the structure statement should follow the key message and show how the subsequent sections *support* that message, not just enumerate them.

3. **Headings Lack Parallelism and Hierarchical Logic**  
   – The six headings are not uniformly phrased as actions or outcomes, which undermines the required parallelism:  
     • “Background” (a context statement)  
     • “Principles of the project‑team approach” (a conceptual statement)  
     • “Definition of project work” (a definitional statement)  
     • “Organization of the program” (an implementation statement)  
     • “Distinctive benefits and results” (outcome statement)  
     • “Conditions required for success” (pre‑condition statement)  
   – This mixture of noun‑type headings breaks the parallel structure that Minto recommends for clarity and logical flow.

4. **Inconsistent Granularity Between Sections**  
   – Some headings are broad (“Background”) while others are narrowly focused (“Conditions required for success”). The pyramid calls for a consistent level of detail across sibling headings to maintain a balanced hierarchy.

5. **Absence of a Logical Narrative Flow**  
   – The order of the headings does not follow a clear logical progression from situation → complication → question → answer (or from context → analysis → recommendation). For example, “Distinctive benefits and results” appears before “Conditions required for success,” which can confuse the reader about whether benefits are presented before the prerequisites for achieving them.

6. **No Explicit “So What?” Statement**  
   – The opening fails to answer the reader’s implicit question, “Why should I care?” A Minto‑compliant introduction would include a concise “so what” that links the described profit‑improvement approach to the reader’s interests or decision‑making needs.
