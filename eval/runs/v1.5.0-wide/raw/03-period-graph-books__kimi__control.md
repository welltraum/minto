## Audit: Period Graph Books

**Governing thought is buried.** The memo's real message — the production process is fragmented, must be streamlined, and the current team cannot do that while also producing the books — appears only in the final paragraph. Minto requires the answer at the top; here the reader must wade through the production workflow before learning why it matters.

**No introduction structure (Situation–Complication–Question–Answer).** The opening states a fact ("Since Period 5, Financial Analysis has been responsible…") but never frames the situation the reader already knows, the complication (errors and inconsistencies appearing), or the question management actually asked (how do we correct this?). Because the question is never posed, the memo can never answer it.

**Unrequested narrative before the point.** The four production steps are presented as freestanding background. The reader hasn't asked "how is it produced?"; they asked "why are there errors?" The steps only earn their place as support for the fragmentation argument — they should appear under it, not ahead of it.

**Findings listed without grouping or summary.** "Problems include inconsistent data…, incorrect variable-cost calculations…, unexplained changes…, prior-period data re-entered" is an ungrouped list. Minto requires ideas at one level to be of the same kind, MECE-grouped, and capped by a sentence stating what they collectively show (e.g., all are symptoms of uncontrolled multi-party data entry). The insight "the same information may be entered three times" is scattered mid-paragraph instead of serving as the summary of the error group.

**Description instead of recommendation.** The task was corrective proposals; the memo delivers an audit narrative and ends with "Could we discuss how best to proceed?" — an invitation, not a recommendation. There is no proposed action, no assignment of the fix, no next step for the reader to approve.

**What must change**

- Open with S-C-Q-A: we took over the books (S); errors and inconsistencies have appeared (C); what must be done to eliminate them (Q); streamline the process and assign the redesign to someone not carrying production (A) — stated in the first paragraph.
- Reorder top-down: governing thought first, then the grouped reasoning, then the production steps as evidence of fragmentation.
- Group the issues into MECE categories (redundant re-entry across parties; no end-to-end data ownership; unclear responsibility between stages) and give each group a summary sentence.
- Close with a specific request — approval to reorganize the process and to staff the change effort separately — not an open-ended offer to discuss.
