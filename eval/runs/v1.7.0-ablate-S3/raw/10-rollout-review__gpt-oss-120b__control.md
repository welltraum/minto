**FIELDBOOK ROLLOUT REVIEW — RECOMMENDATION FOR Q1 EXTENSION**

**RECOMMENDATION**

**Extend Fieldbook to North and Islands in Q1, provided we restructure onboarding to ensure rapid adoption and deploy three integration fixes before go-live.**

The rollout to four regions demonstrates strong operational gains and positive economics in Central and East. However, adoption lag in South and West, combined with data defects causing rework and warranty leakage, threatens margins in new regions. These risks are fully addressable through immediate corrective actions. The vendor relationship is strong, and solutions for known gaps are ready pending our decisions. Extending now captures value while the platform is fresh, assuming we implement the following prerequisites.

---

### 1. EXTENSION IS FINANCIALLY JUSTIFIED AND OPERATIONALLY PROVEN

**Operational efficiency gains are significant and measurable.**
*   **Productivity:** Completed jobs per technician rose from 4.6 to 5.3 per day via route-aware assignment.
*   **Travel:** Average travel time between jobs fell 18%.
*   **Responsiveness:** Same-day emergency insertion is now automatic with a median resolution time of 4 minutes.
*   **Dispatch:** All 41 dispatchers adopted scheduling within week one due to the mandatory switch-off of the legacy spreadsheet.

**Cost savings exceed licensing costs in mature regions.**
*   **Savings:** Overtime spending dropped 12% quarter-over-quarter; fuel cost per job fell 9%, consistent with travel reductions.
*   **Payback:** Central and East paid back their running costs within the second quarter.
*   **Current Run-Rate:** Licences and hosting total £31,000 per month for the four live regions.

**Vendor partnership supports scalable deployment.**
*   **Performance:** Response times are within contract; relationship is constructive.
*   **Readiness:** Vendor solutions exist for all known system gaps and are waiting on Kestrel decisions rather than vendor development.
*   **Feasibility:** IT confirms that required connectors for the asset register and invoicing system are straightforward to implement.

### 2. CURRENT ROLLOUT FLAWS POSE MARGIN RISKS THAT MUST BE ADDRESSED

**Training structure caused adoption lag in South and West.**
*   **Root Cause:** Classroom courses were scheduled only once per region. Technicians hired after the session or absent during it learned second-hand. In West, 34 of 118 technicians never attended any session.
*   **Impact:** South took 11 weeks to pass 80% order volume; West remains at 71% after nine weeks. Coordinators improvised shadowing arrangements, consuming an estimated 380 technician-hours of lost route time.
*   **Financial Consequence:** Slower adoption delays payback. South and West are currently at break-even versus full profitability in Central and East. Repeating this pattern in North and Islands would put the extension underwater for at least two quarters.
*   **Support Burden:** 44% of support tickets are password resets concentrated among technicians who missed classroom onboarding. The support desk is effectively performing training functions at ticket costs.

**Data defects create rework, errors, and warranty leakage.**
*   **Missing Service History:** Fieldbook does not receive equipment history from the legacy asset register.
    *   *Consequence:* Technicians cannot verify warranty status, leading to a 9% rate of unnecessary part replacements under warranty in audited jobs. They also increase back-office call volume.
    *   *Status:* Request for nightly export was made in month two but remains in the asset register team's backlog.
*   **Invoicing Code Mismatch:** Job-completion codes in Fieldbook do not match the invoicing system.
    *   *Consequence:* Billing re-keys roughly 300 records per week, introducing transcription errors caught by customers. A temporary clerk (£8,400 for two quarters) was added to manage this. Reporting modules and invoicing disagree on completed-job counts by 2–4% weekly, requiring manual finance reconciliation.
    *   *Status:* Vendor offers a configurable mapping table solvable in one day, but no internal owner has been assigned.
*   **Duplicate Customer Records:** Search logic matches on exact postcode only, causing technicians to create duplicate records.
    *   *Consequence:* Support merges ~70 duplicates per week manually. Affected customers have split job histories, hindering service visibility.
    *   *Status:* Search logic requires correction.

### 3. IMMEDIATE ACTIONS WILL SECURE SUCCESSFUL EXTENSION

**Restructure onboarding to replicate Central and East performance.**
*   **Shift to Continuous Model:** Replace single classroom sessions with a rolling schedule including weekly webinars and targeted shadowing. This ensures new hires are onboarded immediately and reduces reliance on ad-hoc colleague training.
*   **Target Outcome:** Achieve Central/East adoption pace (full activation within six weeks) in North and Islands.
*   **Benefit:** Eliminates shadowing costs, reduces support ticket volume, and accelerates payback to match profitable regions.

**Deploy vendor solutions for identified integration gaps.**
*   **Assign Integration Ownership:** Designate an internal owner to execute the invoicing code mapping table immediately. This stops re-keying, eliminates transcription errors, and removes the need for the temporary clerk.
*   **Schedule Asset Register Export:** Prioritize the nightly export request in the asset register team's backlog to restore equipment history visibility and prevent warranty leakage.
*   **Fix Search Logic:** Update search parameters to prevent duplicate customer record creation, reducing support merge load and restoring complete job-history views.
*   **Timeline:** IT confirms these fixes are low-effort. Execution should occur prior to Q1 go-live to prevent recurrence of rollout defects.

**Budget for extension costs.**
*   **Incremental Costs:** Extension adds £13,500 per month in licences and requires winter travel for classroom/webinar delivery.
*   **Economic Outlook:** With onboarding restructured and integrations resolved, the economics of extension are favourable. The program office recommends proceeding in Q1 to capitalize on current momentum and vendor readiness.
