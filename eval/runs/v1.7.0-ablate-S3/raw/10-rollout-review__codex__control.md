# Fieldbook Rollout Review — Q1 Extension Decision

## Recommendation

Extend Fieldbook to North and Islands in Q1, but only after assigning ownership and completing three rollout prerequisites: continuous onboarding, asset-history integration, and invoicing-code mapping.

The four-region rollout demonstrates strong operational and financial potential. The conditions that slowed South and West—and would make a Q1 extension uneconomic—are understood and controllable.

## Why extension is justified

Fieldbook has delivered material operating gains in the live regions.

- Completed jobs per technician per day increased from 4.6 to 5.3.
- Travel time between jobs fell 18%.
- Same-day emergency jobs can be inserted in a median of four minutes.
- Overtime fell 12% quarter over quarter and fuel cost per job fell 9%.
- Central and East recovered running costs within the second quarter.

These results support extending the platform, provided North and Islands achieve the faster adoption pattern seen in Central and East. The added licence cost would be £13,500 per month; a repeat of West’s adoption pattern would leave the new regions underwater for at least two quarters.

## What must change before the Q1 rollout

### 1. Replace one-off classroom training with continuous onboarding

Slow adoption resulted primarily from technicians missing the single scheduled classroom course.

- Central and East reached full adoption within six weeks.
- South took eleven weeks to exceed 80% of orders in Fieldbook; West remains at 71% after nine weeks.
- In West, 34 of 118 technicians attended no session.
- Missed training drives support demand: 44% of the 1,940 tickets were login or password issues, concentrated among untrained technicians.
- Informal shadowing in South and West cost an estimated 380 technician-hours of route time.

Before go-live, establish mandatory initial training, catch-up sessions for absentees and new hires, and supported first-login windows. The weekly support webinar should supplement—not replace—onboarding.

### 2. Resolve the two integration gaps and assign a single owner

Missing integrations are consuming operational capacity, causing avoidable errors, and obscuring equipment history.

- Technicians cannot see legacy equipment-service history, contributing to warranty-replacement errors in 9% of audited jobs.
- Billing manually re-keys roughly 300 job records each week because completion codes do not align with invoicing codes; this required a temporary clerk costing £8,400 over two quarters.
- Manual re-keying causes a 2–4% weekly difference between Fieldbook and invoicing completed-job counts.
- The customer-search limitation creates about 70 duplicate records each week, fragmenting customer job histories until support merges them.

Schedule the vendor’s configurable completion-code mapping, evaluate and implement the supported asset-register connector, and assign one Kestrel integration owner with authority to coordinate IT, finance, operations, and the vendor. The vendor’s solutions are available; the constraint is internal ownership and prioritisation.

### 3. Use the rollout as a repeatable program, not a sequence of regional deployments

The program observed the same issues by week four of the first go-live but did not convert them into program-wide fixes. As a result, later regions repeated known problems.

For North and Islands, use a standard readiness gate covering:

- trained and activated technicians;
- integration ownership, scope, and delivery dates;
- local support coverage during the first weeks;
- adoption, data-quality, and billing-reconciliation measures;
- a weekly cross-region review empowered to correct issues before the next deployment milestone.

## Residual issues to manage

The parts-ordering screen remains underused, but testing indicates this is a habit-change issue rather than a product defect. Address it through field coaching after core adoption and integration readiness are secured.

Customer-record duplication should be tracked as a data-quality issue and escalated to the vendor for a search improvement, but it should not delay extension if the core readiness conditions above are met.

## Decision requested

Approve Q1 extension to North and Islands conditional on completion of the three prerequisites before go-live:

1. Continuous onboarding and supported activation process in place.
2. Named integration owner, with completion-code mapping scheduled and asset-history connector implementation committed.
3. Program readiness gate and weekly corrective-governance process established.
