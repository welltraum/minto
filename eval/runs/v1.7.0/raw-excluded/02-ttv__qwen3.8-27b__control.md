Subject: TTW composing costs — recommended actions

Key line
TTW should reduce composing costs by (1) piloting simplified quality-control checks for inexpensive titles and (2) conducting a methods study to address its 20-50% productivity gap. The pilot could save up to 10% of total composing cost; the methods study could identify further savings.

Support
1. The cost exposure is material.
   - Composition is the largest production cost, at roughly 40% of hardback cost and 50-55% of paperback cost.
   - TTW is uncompetitive on simple work and does not currently know whether its composing costs are excessive.

2. Simplified controls can reduce cost on inexpensive titles.
   - Complex reference books and simple novels currently pass through essentially the same quality-control stages.
   - TTW should test selected inexpensive jobs by removing or retiming checks and measuring the effect on quality and customers.
   - The saving could reach 10% of total composing cost.

3. A methods study can address the productivity gap.
   - Industry comparisons show TTW trails the benchmark by 20-50%, indicating weak productivity and high cost.
   - A separate methods study could identify further productivity improvements by changing the composing method.

4. Management support makes the next steps feasible.
   - Managers disagree about whether costs are high, but all are willing to investigate.
   - George Kennedy is willing to test whether some stages can be removed and why measured productivity trails the benchmark.

Caveat
- Comparisons with three other printers may provide useful context, although a simple comparison is unlikely to settle the issue.

Omitted or demoted
- The two-week scope, the general Aylesbury interest in causes, and the names of Roy Walter and Brian Thompson were omitted because they do not support the recommendation.
- Operational staffing and labour issues—department overload, manual-composition capacity shortage, pay below local market rates, hiring and retention problems, the loss of two employees, late jobs, overtime more than 50% over budget, and the union claim—were omitted from the main argument because the recommended actions do not address them. They should be handled in a separate operations/HR review if required.
