## Audit — "Period Graph Books"

**Reader:** senior management, who asked for corrective proposals.
**Question they hold:** *What should we do about the errors in the graph books?*
**Kind of answer that belongs on top:** actions — the changes to make and who makes them. The document delivers a process description and a diagnosis instead, and ends by asking for a meeting.

### Legend

### Findings

| Location | Violation | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Subject + para. 1 | No answer anywhere in the document. The reader asked *what to do*; the memo never states an action to take. This is not a misplaced answer — it is a missing one. | Top must be the recommendation: streamline production under single ownership, with the resources to do it. |
| Closing line | The ask is "could we discuss", so the decision is handed back to the reader. | State what you need: approval to redesign, and staffing for the transition. |
| "Overall assessment" | Buried logic: the controlling idea (fragmentation, no end-to-end owner), the recommendation (streamline), and the ask (the team cannot do both) all sit at the bottom. | Promote all three above the evidence; everything else becomes support. |
| "Production" section | Wrong kind on the first level: four steps of current mechanics standing where reasons or actions belong. | Move to an appendix, or hang under the branch it proves ("three re-entries of the same data"). |
| "Issues" section | Mixed kinds in one group — a cause, a process narrative, and a symptom list. | Split: fragmentation is the cause and becomes a branch; the symptoms become supports beneath it. |
| Symptom list vs. "Production" step 3 | Overlap: the re-entry cycle is described twice under two headings. | One home for it, with all its evidence gathered there. |
| Symptom list | Gap: four defect classes named with no measurement of frequency, cost, or which books are affected. | Attach evidence per class, or mark `[data needed]` — a claim of severity with no scale cannot carry a resource request. |

### The three fixes that buy the most

1. Put the recommendation on top: consolidate production of all four books under one owner and one data path — and say what that costs.
2. Convert the first level from *description* to *actions*: what changes, who owns each change, by when. Current mechanics drop to appendix.
3. Replace "could we discuss" with the specific decision required — sign-off plus interim resourcing, since the team cannot redesign and produce simultaneously.
