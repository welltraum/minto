**SUBJECT:** Recommendation to Extend Fieldbook to North and Islands in Q1

**RECOMMENDATION**

Extend Fieldbook to the North and Islands regions in Q1, provided we restructure onboarding to guarantee full technician coverage and resolve critical integration gaps before go-live. The business case is positive, risks are known and solvable, and program improvements will ensure successful replication of the Central and East results.

**1. EXTENSION DELIVERS STRONG FINANCIAL RETURNS AND OPERATIONAL GAINS**

*   **Productivity gains are proven and significant.**
    *   Route-aware assignment increased completed jobs per technician from 4.6 to 5.3 across live regions.
    *   Average travel time between jobs fell 18%, enabling same-day emergency insertions to be automated with a median response time of 4 minutes.
    *   Fieldbook is now the source of record for job status; regional managers rely on its dashboard for Monday reviews.
*   **Variable cost reductions improve margin.**
    *   Overtime spending in live regions decreased 12% quarter over quarter.
    *   Fuel cost per job dropped 9%, consistent with travel-time reductions.
    *   These savings confirm the platform's ability to lower operating expenses at scale.
*   **Return on investment is validated in high-adoption regions.**
    *   Central and East reached full adoption within six weeks and paid back their share of running costs within the second quarter.
    *   Extension would add £13,500 per month in licences and require winter travel for training; however, economics remain favourable if the new regions replicate the Central/East adoption pace.
    *   South and West are currently at break-even due to slower adoption, demonstrating that execution quality, not platform performance, drives variance.

**2. KEY RISKS ARE IDENTIFIABLE AND RESOLVABLE PRIOR TO LAUNCH**

*   **Adoption lag stems from training scheduling gaps, which can be corrected.**
    *   South and West lagged because the two-day classroom course was scheduled only once per region; technicians hired after the course or absent during it learned second-hand, causing West to reach only 71% adoption after nine weeks.
    *   In West, 34 of 118 technicians never attended any session, forcing improvised shadowing that consumed an estimated 380 hours of lost route time.
    *   *Mitigation:* Restructure onboarding to provide multiple training cohorts aligned with hiring cycles. Dispatcher adoption reached 100% immediately via forced migration, proving that access controls can drive compliance when training coverage is assured.
*   **Integration defects cause rework and errors, which can be closed with vendor tools.**
    *   Missing service history from the legacy asset register forces technicians to call the back office or ask customers, resulting in unnecessary part replacements in 9% of audited jobs and hiding warranty states.
    *   Mismatched job-completion codes require the billing team to manually re-key roughly 300 records per week, introducing transcription errors and causing a 2–4% weekly discrepancy between Fieldbook and invoicing systems.
    *   *Mitigation:* The vendor catalogue includes supported connectors for both the asset register and invoicing import format. IT confirms these are straightforward to implement; the blocker is lack of scope and ownership, not technical complexity.
*   **Support burden reflects onboarding and configuration gaps, not product defects.**
    *   44% of support tickets are password resets concentrated among technicians who missed classroom training; 19% are duplicate customer records caused by a search limitation matching on exact postcodes only.
    *   The support desk is performing training functions at ticket costs, and duplicate merging consumes 70 records per week.
    *   *Mitigation:* Comprehensive onboarding will eliminate login-related tickets. The search limitation and code-mapping table are configurable fixes awaiting Kestrel decisions.

**3. PROGRAM IMPROVEMENTS ENABLE SUCCESSFUL REPLICATION**

*   **Feedback mechanisms must be instituted to accelerate fixes.**
    *   Every negative pattern observed in this review was visible by week four of the Central go-live, yet the same issues replayed in subsequent regions due to a lack of mechanism to convert field observations into program-level actions.
    *   *Action:* Implement a formal process to capture regional learnings and deploy fixes before the next region launches.
*   **Vendor partnership supports rapid resolution of pending items.**
    *   The vendor maintains strong responsiveness within contract terms, and solutions for both the asset register feed and invoicing mapping are ready pending Kestrel approval.
    *   *Action:* Assign an integration owner immediately to schedule the mapping table setup and scope the connectors, leveraging the vendor's readiness to minimize delay.
*   **Dispatchers demonstrate that adoption is achievable with decisive measures.**
    *   All 41 dispatchers adopted Fieldbook scheduling from week one because the legacy spreadsheet was switched off on day one.
    *   *Action:* Apply similar decisive access controls for North and Islands to ensure scheduling compliance while training catches up with technician onboarding.
