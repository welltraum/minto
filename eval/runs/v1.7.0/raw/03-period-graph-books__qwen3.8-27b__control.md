# Audit: Period Graph Books

## Overall finding

The document does not follow the Minto Pyramid Principle. It is organized as a descriptive account of the current process followed by a loosely related set of problems and a late general assessment. It does not lead with a governing thought, organize the issues into a coherent key line, or make the corrective proposal and decision request expected by senior management.

---

## Defects and required changes

### 1. No answer-first governing thought

**Defect:**  
The opening says the memo “describes issues that appeared after the transfer.” It does not state the main conclusion, diagnosis, or recommended corrective action. Management asked for corrective proposals, but the document does not answer the implied question.

**Must change:**  
The document must begin with a single controlling statement that captures the essential problem and the recommended direction for correction. The first paragraph should give the answer, not merely the background.

---

### 2. Missing or incomplete SCQA frame

**Defect:**  
The situation — Financial Analysis took over production — and the complication — errors and inconsistencies — are present, but the question and answer are not made explicit.

**Must change:**  
Make the implicit question explicit: what should be done about the errors and inconsistencies? Then answer it at the top of the document.

---

### 3. Process description substitutes for argument

**Defect:**  
The “Production” section lists operational steps without stating what those steps prove. The steps are not tied to a claim such as excessive handoffs, duplicate entry, weak control, or lack of accountability.

**Must change:**  
Either remove the step-by-step process from the main argument, reduce it to a brief illustration, or move it to an appendix. Any retained process detail must directly support a higher-level point.

---

### 4. Issues are not grouped into a key line

**Defect:**  
The “Issues” section mixes root causes, symptoms, examples, and implementation constraints. Items such as fragmented process, multiple entries, inconsistent data, incorrect calculations, unexplained changes, re-entry, and team capacity are not organized into a small number of major supporting points.

**Must change:**  
Create a key line of 3–5 major points that are mutually exclusive and collectively exhaustive. The issues should be grouped into distinct categories, such as data ownership/control, duplicate transcription, calculation integrity, period consistency, and change-management capacity.

---

### 5. Horizontal logic is weak

**Defect:**  
Several points overlap or operate at different levels of abstraction. “Fragmented process,” “three corporate departments participate,” “entered three times,” “entered again,” “no one controls the data,” and “responsibility is unclear” repeat the same underlying concern. Symptoms and causes are not separated.

**Must change:**  
Ensure each supporting point is distinct, parallel, and at the same level of abstraction. Separate causes from effects, and avoid restating the same idea in different words.

---

### 6. Vertical logic is broken

**Defect:**  
The headings and paragraphs do not form a clean question-answer chain. “Production” raises the question “so what?” “Issues” raises “which issues matter most and why?” “Overall assessment” arrives after the reader has already received the details.

**Must change:**  
Arrange the document so each lower level answers the question raised by the level above. The top message should generate the need for the supporting groups, and each group should contain evidence or subpoints that justify it.

---

### 7. The conclusion is buried and underdeveloped

**Defect:**  
The closest thing to a governing thought is in “Overall assessment,” but it appears after the process and problem details. It is also vague: “badly needs streamlining” and “required changes” are not defined.

**Must change:**  
Promote the core conclusion to the top and develop it with specific supporting points. The conclusion should be assertive, specific, and sufficient to guide the next decision.

---

### 8. No clear corrective proposal or decision ask

**Defect:**  
The document ends with “Could we discuss how best to proceed?” This is an open-ended request for discussion, not a proposal or decision request. It does not tell management what choice is being requested, what options exist, or what immediate next step is proposed.

**Must change:**  
State the decision sought, the preferred corrective approach, any alternatives considered, the immediate next step, the responsible owner, and the timing. The ask should be actionable.

---

### 9. Insufficient evidence and impact

**Defect:**  
The memo asserts that errors and inconsistencies have appeared and that the process is cumbersome, but it does not quantify or prioritize the impact. Terms such as “most problems” and “badly needs” are unsupported.

**Must change:**  
Tie each major issue to a concrete consequence — rework, delay, incorrect reporting, management risk, or cost — and prioritize issues by materiality or frequency.

---

### 10. Audience focus is operational rather than managerial

**Defect:**  
Senior management is given a detailed production sequence and a list of operational symptoms, but not a clear management-level implication or choice.

**Must change:**  
Focus the main body on the management-relevant conclusion, risks, and decision. Keep operational detail minimal and subordinate to the argument.

---

### 11. Subject/title does not carry the message

**Defect:**  
“Period Graph Books” identifies the topic only. It does not communicate the main message, problem, or requested decision.

**Must change:**  
The title or opening line should convey the main message or decision, not just the subject area.

---

## Summary of required structural change

The document must move from:

Background → process steps → mixed issues → late assessment → open discussion

to:

Governing thought → key line of grouped supporting points → evidence/examples → recommended corrective action → explicit decision ask.
