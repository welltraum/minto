## Minto Pyramid Audit

**Overall judgment:** The memo identifies real operational failures, but it is not yet a persuasive pyramid. It ends with a vague request to discuss rather than a clear governing recommendation and supporting case.

### Defects

- **No clear answer at the top.** The “overall assessment” says the process needs streamlining, but does not state the decision management should make, the proposed owner, or the required resources.

- **The situation is incomplete.** The transfer of responsibility is stated, but the operating requirement for the graph books—accuracy, consistency, timeliness, and reliable senior-management reporting—is not established.

- **The complication is a list, not a defined problem.** Multiple data entry, calculation errors, inconsistencies, and unclear responsibility are intermixed. The reader must infer the central issue: no end-to-end controlled production process exists.

- **The question is weak and open-ended.** “Could we discuss how best to proceed?” does not create a decision-oriented question. It leaves management to diagnose and design the response.

- **The main ideas are not mutually exclusive.** Process fragmentation, repeated entry, calculation errors, data inconsistency, and responsibility gaps overlap causally. They should be grouped into distinct, parallel reasons supporting the recommendation.

- **The production steps are too detailed for their position.** The procedure is evidence, but it appears before the governing message and without showing which steps create which failure.

- **Evidence is not organized under conclusions.** Examples from one book and the broader assessment are not tied to explicit supporting points such as data integrity, process ownership, and implementation capacity.

- **The final claim introduces an unsupported capacity conclusion.** The assertion that the current team cannot both change the process and maintain production needs evidence or should be framed as a resourcing requirement.

### What must change

- Lead with a specific management action, not a request for discussion.
- Build a Situation–Complication–Question–Answer opening that frames the required decision.
- Define the core problem as an end-to-end control and ownership failure.
- Support the answer with a small set of parallel, non-overlapping reasons.
- Move process detail and error examples beneath the reason each proves.
- Distinguish immediate production safeguards from the longer-term redesign and resourcing decision.
- State the accountable owner, scope across all four books, and the decision or support required from management.
