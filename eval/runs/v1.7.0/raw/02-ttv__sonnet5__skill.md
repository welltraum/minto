Subject: Two changes can cut composing cost — both need a two-month test

Composition is our largest production cost — 40% for hardbacks, 50-55% for paperbacks — and we are uncompetitive on simple work. You asked me to find out why. Two causes stand out, and both point to a test we should run now.

Situation: Composition is TTW's largest production cost, and every title — complex reference book or simple novel — goes through the same quality-control stages.

Complication: TTW is losing simple work on price, productivity trails the industry benchmark by 20-50%, and the composing department is now overloaded — short-staffed after losing two compositors, running most jobs late, with overtime over 50% above budget and a union claim pending.

Question: How can TTW bring composing costs down?

Answer: Cut composing cost two ways — simplify the process on inexpensive titles, and raise productivity in manual composition — starting with a controlled test of both.

1. Simplify the process for inexpensive titles
   - Run selected simple jobs through fewer, differently timed quality checks and measure the effect on quality and customer acceptance.
   - Potential saving: roughly 10% of total composing cost.

2. Raise manual composing productivity
   - Commission a methods study of the composing process itself; TTW's productivity trails the benchmark by 20-50%, so the room to close is large.
   - George Kennedy has agreed to run this test and diagnose why measured productivity trails the benchmark.

Next step: authorize the two-month test of both changes; Kennedy is ready to start.
