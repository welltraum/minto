Audit findings:

- The opening lacks a governing thought. It states the memorandum’s purpose (“describes how…”) rather than giving the main conclusion, recommendation, or answer.
- “It is organized in six sections” makes the setout a table of contents rather than a pyramid of supporting ideas.
- The six headings are a flat, ungrouped list; they do not visibly form a single logical group under a central thought.
- The headings do not visibly answer the question implied by the first sentence (“how…”). They mix context, definition, method, outcomes, and prerequisites.
- “Background” is contextual framing, not a supporting idea in the answer pyramid.
- The headings are not consistently parallel or at the same level of abstraction.
- “Distinctive benefits and results” is double-barreled, combining two related but distinct ideas in one heading.
- The visible order is not clearly logical; “Principles of the project-team approach” precedes “Definition of project work,” and benefits are listed before the conditions required for success.
- There is no visible inductive or deductive relationship among the headings.
- Terminology shifts between “project teams,” “project work,” and “program,” creating ambiguity in the visible opening.
