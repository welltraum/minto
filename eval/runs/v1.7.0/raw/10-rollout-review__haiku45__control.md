# FIELDBOOK ROLLOUT — TWO-QUARTER REVIEW

**RECOMMENDATION: EXTEND TO NORTH AND ISLANDS IN Q1, PROVIDED ONBOARDING IS RESTRUCTURED**

Fieldbook has delivered substantial operational gains where adoption succeeded. Extension makes business sense—but only if training is fixed. Adoption depends entirely on onboarding design, not platform quality. Central and East reached full adoption in six weeks via structured classroom training before launch; South and West stalled at 71-80% due to single one-time sessions that missed later hires. Integration gaps blocking further gains are vendor-ready but awaiting Kestrel prioritization. If North and Islands replicate Central/East onboarding, the ROI timeline is Q2 payback; if they repeat South/West, regions go underwater for two quarters.

---

## 1. ADOPTION SUCCESS IS REPRODUCIBLE IF TRAINING IS RESTRUCTURED

Central and East prove the platform works. South and West show that single-session classroom training fails.

**Central/East (six-week full adoption):**
- Classroom training delivered before launch reached technicians before first use
- 100% dispatcher adoption immediate (backup spreadsheet switched off day one)
- Support tickets peaked week three, then declined steadily

**South/West (71-80% after eleven weeks):**
- Single classroom session per region meant technicians hired after training or absent during it learned from colleagues
- In West, 34 of 118 technicians never attended training
- Support desk: 1,940 tickets over two quarters; 44% password resets and login problems from untrained technicians
- Regional coordinators improvised peer shadowing (effective but cost 380 technician-hours of lost route time)

**For North and Islands:**
Implement Central/East model: multiple training sessions scheduled after hire, mandatory attendance before system access. Supplement with weekly webinar (currently 20–30 attendees), but do not rely on it as primary onboarding.

---

## 2. OPERATIONAL GAINS ARE PROVEN; INTEGRATION GAPS ARE RESOLVABLE

**Measurable gains across four live regions:**
- Jobs per technician per day: +15% (4.6 to 5.3)
- Travel time: –18%
- Same-day emergency response: automatic (4-minute median vs. coordinator phone tag)
- Overtime: –12% quarter-over-quarter
- Fuel cost per job: –9%

**Payback achieved in Central/East by Q2; South/West at break-even.**

**Two integration issues with vendor solutions waiting on Kestrel decisions:**

1. **Asset register data missing from job screens** (9% of jobs have duplicate parts replaced under warranty)
   - Vendor solution: nightly export
   - Status: requested month two, in IT backlog
   - Required: assign integration owner

2. **Job-completion codes misaligned with invoicing** (300 records/week manually re-keyed by billing team)
   - Vendor solution: configurable mapping table (~1 day setup)
   - Status: no integration owner assigned
   - Required: assign owner, configure mapping

Both issues are post-launch corrections, not deployment blockers. Neither requires vendor development.

---

## 3. ECONOMICS FAVOR EXTENSION IF ADOPTION MATCHES CENTRAL/EAST PACE

Current cost: £31,000/month for four regions. Extension: +£13,500/month.

**Two-quarter financial outcome:**
- Central/East: cost recovered in Q2 through productivity gains
- South/West: break-even due to slower adoption and training overhead (£8,400 temporary billing clerk, 380 technician-hours lost route time)

**Extension scenarios:**
- Strong adoption (Central/East model): ROI positive Q2 onward
- Weak adoption (South/West model): underwater for two quarters minimum

**Economics gate:** Training design determines adoption curve. Fix training, extension is sound.

---

## 4. PROGRAM GOVERNANCE: PREVENT SOUTH/WEST PATTERNS FROM REPEATING

- Every negative pattern was visible by week four in Central but was not systematized; identical issues replayed in South and West
- Both integration gaps are waiting on Kestrel prioritization, not vendor work

**For North and Islands:**
- Assign integration owner before launch (asset register, job-code mapping)
- Establish weekly cross-region review to escalate issues week one, not week four
- These prevent the South/West cost and delay pattern

---

**DECISION GATES FOR Q1 EXTENSION**

1. Onboarding restructured to Central/East model (multiple sessions, mandatory attendance before access)
2. Integration owner assigned for asset-register and job-code feeds
3. Weekly cross-region review established

With these conditions met, extension carries proven economics and operational upside with known risks managed.
