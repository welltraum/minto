## Audit: Fixture 07 — opening and setout

**Verdict:** The opening does not satisfy the Pyramid Principle. It announces a table of contents rather than a governing idea, and the six headings do not form a logical grouping under any such idea.

### 1. No governing thought at the top
The first sentence states the document's *subject* ("how project teams identify and deliver significant profit improvements"), not its *answer*. A reader finishes the opening knowing what the memorandum is about but not what it asserts. Nothing here can be supported, disputed, or acted on.

### 2. The setout is a list of categories, not a set of points
Each heading is a noun phrase — a container label. None makes a statement, so none can be a summary of the section beneath it. The reader cannot preview the argument by reading the headings, which is the test a Minto setout must pass.

### 3. The six headings are not a valid grouping
They are heterogeneous in kind and mixed in level of abstraction:
- *Background* is context, not part of the answer; in the pyramid it belongs in the introduction, not as a peer of the substantive sections.
- *Principles of the project-team approach* and *Definition of project work* both describe what the approach **is**, overlapping rather than dividing cleanly.
- *Organization of the program* describes **how** it is run.
- *Distinctive benefits and results* describes **what it produces** — an effect, sitting at a different logical level from the descriptive headings above it.
- *Conditions required for success* describes **prerequisites** — a qualification on the whole, not a component of it.

Mixing description, mechanism, outcome, and precondition means the set is neither MECE nor a single deductive or inductive line. There is no plural noun that names what these six things collectively are.

### 4. No discernible logical order
The sequence follows neither time, structure, nor degree of importance. Because the headings are labels rather than statements, no ordering principle is even detectable from the setout.

### 5. Missing introduction structure
There is no Situation–Complication–Question sequence. Without it, the reader is given no reason the memorandum exists and no question for the six sections to answer — so the sections cannot be judged relevant or complete.

### 6. "Six sections" signals structure chosen before argument
Announcing the count foregrounds the filing scheme. A pyramid derives its divisions from the logic of the answer; the number of sections is a consequence, not a feature to advertise.

### Not assessed
The body is unavailable. This audit covers only the opening sentence and the setout; whether each section's content in fact supports a defensible point cannot be determined here.
