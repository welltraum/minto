**FIELD BOOK ROLLOUT REVIEW — RECOMMENDATION FOR Q1 EXTENSION**

**Recommendation:** Extend Fieldbook to North and Islands in Q1, contingent on restructuring onboarding, activating two integrations, and establishing a rapid feedback loop.

### 1. The business case supports expansion if adoption targets are maintained
*   **Operating savings currently exceed license costs; payback is achieved in high-adoption regions.**
    *   Overtime spending is down 12% quarter-over-quarter; fuel cost per job is down 9%.
    *   Central and East regions paid back running costs within the second quarter.
    *   Current run rate is £31,000/month for four regions; unplanned costs (e.g., temporary billing clerk, shadowing losses) are directly linked to adoption gaps in South and West.
*   **Extension economics remain favorable provided adoption pace matches Central and East.**
    *   Extension adds £13,500/month in licenses and requires winter travel for classroom training.
    *   Program office analysis indicates the economics are favourable if and only if the Central/East adoption curve is reproduced.
*   **Financial risk is isolated to adoption failure; low adoption delays payback significantly.**
    *   South and West are roughly at break-even due to slower adoption.
    *   A repeat of the West pattern in new regions would put operations underwater for at least two quarters.

### 2. Operational gains are proven, scalable, and drive significant efficiency
*   **Technician productivity and route efficiency have improved materially.**
    *   Completed jobs per technician per day rose from 4.6 to 5.3 (+15%).
    *   Average travel time between jobs fell 18%; same-day emergency insertions are now automatic with a 4-minute median resolution.
*   **User adoption is strong and dispatcher compliance is complete.**
    *   412 of 486 field technicians (85%) have activated accounts; 83% of work orders are created and closed in Fieldbook.
    *   All 41 dispatchers adopted scheduling by week one following removal of the legacy spreadsheet.
    *   Parts-ordering friction is attributed to habit rather than system defect.
*   **Scaling potential is confirmed by early-adopter performance.**
    *   Central and East reached full adoption within six weeks.
    *   Support ticket volume peaked in week three and declined steadily, indicating a stabilizing user base.

### 3. Execution risks are identified and resolvable through immediate program actions
*   **Onboarding bottleneck must be fixed to prevent adoption lag and unplanned costs.**
    *   *Root cause:* Classroom courses were scheduled only once per region; late hires and absentees learned second-hand, forcing experienced technicians into shadowing roles that reduced route time (estimated 380 lost hours in South/West).
    *   *Action:* Restructure training schedule to ensure universal coverage for all hires; eliminate reliance on shadowing arrangements.
*   **Two integration gaps cause operational friction but have vendor-ready solutions.**
    *   *Asset Register Feed:* Missing service history forces technicians to call the back office and results in incorrect part replacements (9% error rate in audited jobs).
        *   *Action:* Activate nightly export; IT confirms feasibility, vendor solution is ready, request was made in month two.
    *   *Code Mapping:* Mismatched job-completion codes require manual billing re-keying (300 records/week), introducing transcription errors and causing 2–4% variance in reporting.
        *   *Action:* Configure mapping table; implementation takes one day, requires assignment of an integration owner.
*   **Program governance must capture early learnings to prevent recurrence.**
    *   *Root cause:* Negative patterns were visible by week four in Central, but the program lacked a mechanism to convert field observations into program-level fixes, causing issues to replay in subsequent regions.
    *   *Action:* Implement a formal feedback loop to escalate and resolve field observations immediately.
    *   *Readiness:* Vendor relationship is strong with response times within contract; both integration solutions await Kestrel decisions rather than vendor work.
