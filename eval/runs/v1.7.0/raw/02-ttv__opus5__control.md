**Subject:** Reducing TTW composing-room costs

TTW can cut composing costs by an estimated 10-25% by simplifying quality control on simple titles and changing composing methods. I recommend we launch two studies now, in parallel.

**1. Simplify the process for inexpensive titles — potential saving up to 10% of total composing cost.**

Every title passes through essentially the same quality-control stages, whether it is a complex reference book or a simple novel. Simple work is where TTW is least competitive, so the checks are most likely to be excess cost there. George Kennedy is willing to run a test: take selected jobs, remove or re-time individual checks, and measure the effect on quality and on customers. That test settles the question in weeks rather than by argument.

**2. Raise productivity by changing the composing method — TTW trails the benchmark by 20-50%.**

Industry comparisons show weak productivity and high cost. A methods study should establish how much of that gap is method and how much is capacity, and specify the changes worth making. The gap is large enough that even partial closure outweighs the saving from item 1.

**Why to act now rather than study further**

The department is already overloaded, and manual composition most of all. TTW pays below local market rates, cannot hire or retain compositors, has just lost two employees, and now runs most jobs late. Overtime is more than 50% over budget and the union is pressing a new claim. Cost pressure is therefore rising while capacity falls; each month of delay adds overtime and late delivery rather than preserving options.

**Recommended next steps**

- Commission Kennedy's quality-control test on a defined sample of simple titles, with agreed quality and customer measures.
- Commission a methods study of manual composition, covering both method and staffing.
- Report both back to Aylesbury management jointly, since the two savings interact.

---

**What I have omitted and why**

- **Benchmarking against three other printers.** Feasible, but a simple comparison will not settle whether TTW's costs are excessive — differences in title mix and quality standards will dominate. The internal test in item 1 answers the question directly and faster.
- **The disagreement among managers about whether costs are high.** Not decision-relevant: all of them, including Roy Walter and Brian Thompson, are willing to investigate, so the disagreement does not block either study.
- **A narrative of the two weeks' interviews and data gathering.** Demoted to supporting material; available on request.
