**Subject Line**
- **Defect:** Topic-based and non-actionable (“Period Graph Books”). Fails to signal purpose, recommendation, or decision required.
- **Required Change:** Replace with a complete sentence stating the core recommendation or key takeaway (e.g., “Recommend Restructuring Graph Book Production to Eliminate Recurring Errors”).

**Opening & Lead Paragraph**
- **Defect:** Opens with historical context and transfer details. Violates the “answer first” principle by delaying the main conclusion until the final section.
- **Required Change:** Begin with the primary recommendation or key diagnosis. Condense background/context into a brief framing statement placed after the lead, ensuring it serves only to set up the argument.

**Structural Organization (Production / Issues / Overall Assessment)**
- **Defect:** Organized chronologically/by handoff sequence rather than by logical argument. The “Production” section functions as a procedural checklist, while the actual problem statement and solution are buried in “Overall assessment.”
- **Required Change:** Reorganize top-down. Place the central recommendation at the apex, followed by 3–4 key supporting reasons. Move detailed process steps and error examples to subordinate tiers as supporting evidence, not as primary headings.

**Logical Grouping & MECE Compliance**
- **Defect:** The “Issues” section mixes root causes, symptoms, and isolated examples without hierarchical separation. Overlapping concepts (e.g., “fragmented process,” “information entered three times,” “no one controls data”) violate Mutually Exclusive, Collectively Exhaustive criteria.
- **Required Change:** Group all supporting points into distinct, non-overlapping categories that collectively address the main recommendation. Apply consistent parallel phrasing and order them logically (e.g., by process stage, impact severity, or frequency).

**Call to Action / Closing**
- **Defect:** Ends with a passive, open-ended question (“Could we discuss how best to proceed?”). Provides no clear decision point, timeline, or proposed next steps.
- **Required Change:** State the exact decision required from senior management, specify the recommended course of action, and propose concrete next steps (e.g., approval to redesign the workflow, assignment of a process owner, or scheduled implementation review).

**Overall Hierarchy & Narrative Flow**
- **Defect:** Follows a bottom-up narrative (context → process description → scattered issues → delayed assessment → vague question). Lacks a single controlling idea that unifies the document.
- **Required Change:** Establish one clear controlling idea at the top. Ensure every lower level directly answers the question raised by the level above it. Remove redundant or tangential details that do not directly support the central recommendation.
